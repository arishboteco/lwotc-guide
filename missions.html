Andy mission cards — additional execution rules v6

Smash & Grab vs Extract Advent Supplies

Smash & Grab: revealed start, building/boxes, predictable fixed RNF. You can often clear enemies with a couple of turns to spare; do not manufacture urgency.

Extract Supplies: concealed WOTC-style crate race; Andy considers it substantially riskier. Put the evac near the last collection area, do not chase distant crates into fog, and delay tagging neutral crates so ADVENT may mark them first and let you reclaim them as a free action at the start of your turn.

Andy’s rough full-haul example is about 75 supplies + 14 alloys + 14 Elerium, but 1.2.3 rebalanced these reward schedules; treat the number as historical/illustrative, not a guaranteed current payout.

Supply Raid / Troop Ambush

Exceptional reward, exceptional wipe potential. Andy generally wants <20 expected enemies, a strong team and good equipment. This is one of the few mission families he will boost, send more than six to, or even pull the A-team from another infiltration for.

Do not hoard rockets/incendiaries when a giant rainbow pod or multiple pods collide. A healthy squad with no consumables is preferable to a wounded squad that “saved” ordinance.

Covert Op Ambush

Always send the covert pair with a medikit. Technicals and Shinobis are strong ambush-risk candidates because Squaddies still bring fire control/scouting. The four-person rescue team values mobility and immediate impact: SPARK, Skirmisher and a Specialist with Medical Protocol can be excellent.

The stranded pair should avoid waking pods until rescuers close the distance; when forced to fight, use control to buy time rather than expecting a B/C-equipment pair to alpha-strike a full pod.

Sabotage Transmitter vs Destroy Relay

Destroy Relay: use concealment to approach and set the ambush; RNF pressure starts after reveal. Sabotage Transmitter: very short objective timer, so take the first decent ambush and use relays to buy time. Do not extend the objective timer so long that the RNF clock kills you.

Blacksite / Facility

Throw evac earlier than intuition suggests; facility evac can be very slow. For Blacksite, Andy prefers clearing the map, staging almost everyone near evac, then sending the fast soldier for the vial because taking the vial starts reinforcement pressure. The 45-turn doom timer is generous: do not rush the map.

UFO / Chosen Avenger Defense

Defense Matrix is extremely valuable: upgraded, staffed turrets can provide eight meaningful shots per turn. On Chosen Defense, do not get sucked into racing the objective; Andy reports enough Avenger power for careful play. The 1.2 version is far more beatable than old LWOTC versions.

Complete mission reference — infiltration, mission-specific quirks, quick response missions & SITREPs

Squad size, over-infiltration and enemy readiness

  • Plan normal missions around roughly five soldiers. Six is comfortable when the timer allows and can carry a trainee. Seven is usually too strategically expensive for ordinary guerrilla ops unless the mission is critical or untimed.
  • Over-infiltration can effectively reduce the mission’s regional-Strength contribution and sometimes enemy detection radius, but the exact breakpoint is not guaranteed to occur in clean 25% steps.
  • Do not leave soldiers tied up for days merely because a 150% infiltration target exists. Launch when the expected enemy count and concealment conditions are acceptable.
  • Always plan for 100% infiltration, but 90–99% can be rational if the displayed enemy count is already where you want it and the mission does not impose a critical sub-100% penalty.

Jailbreak

  • RNF pressure can be tight depending on evac placement. Move faster than on a leisurely hack mission, but still avoid blind activations. Jailbreaks are a major reason Recruit jobs are often unnecessary.

Smash & Grab vs Extract ADVENT Supplies

Smash & Grab

  • Revealed start; several crates concentrated around an ADVENT structure.
  • Usually enough time to clear natural enemies carefully before fixed reinforcements become a serious issue.
  • Each soldier physically carrying a crate matters, so squad positioning and evac route planning are important.

Extract ADVENT Supplies

  • Concealed start; scattered crates and a short post-reveal RNF window.
  • Much more prone to baiting the player into unsafe movement for tagged crates. Do not dash into fog just because ADVENT marked one.
  • Call evac so that the pickup arrives around the first RNF window and center it near the final cluster of crates.
  • Wait to tag neutral crates when possible: if ADVENT marks it on their turn, reclaiming it can become a free action on yours.
  • Specialists are good remote crate collectors when the rear area is already scouted because many of their combat actions work at range.

Destroy Relay vs Sabotage Transmitter

  • Destroy Relay: normal timed-objective logic. RNF pressure begins after concealment breaks, so use concealment to close distance before the first engagement.
  • Sabotage Transmitter: very short objective timer, extended by shooting relay nodes. Break concealment earlier, use pistols/melee for cheap relay destruction, and remember that extending the objective timer does not stop RNF escalation.

Covert Op Ambush

  • Never treat a single-digit ambush chance as zero. Send a team that can survive with C/B-tier equipment and give at least one operative a Medikit.
  • The stranded pair should avoid waking pods until rescuers arrive; if contact happens early, use control (burn/stun) rather than trying to out-damage a full pod.
  • Technical and Shinobi are strong covert operatives because they provide control/scouting even at low rank.
  • The rescue team is capped and should prioritize mobility and immediate impact: SPARK Overdrive, Skirmisher grapple, a combat-capable Specialist, etc. A pure healer who contributes no offense is a poor use of a tiny rescue squad.
  • Ambushes are also valuable no-infiltration XP opportunities; they are not purely a punishment.

Supply Raids / Troop Ambushes

  • High strategic value: resources, corpses and regional Strength reduction. No normal objective timer, but maps are compact enough that 20+ enemies can create multi-pod pileups.
  • Below ~20 enemies and with adequate infiltration, bring the best team you can. These are among the few mission types where more than six soldiers can be justified if the timer supports it.
  • Expect large rainbow pods. Bring AoE control, boss damage and enough ordinance to survive the one turn where multiple pods converge.
  • DFA Sharpshooters can be awkward on flat wilderness maps; concealed Shinobi scouting is valuable but dangerous if the scout gets isolated.
  • The special Covert Op troop ambush is smaller, has a fixed-ish infiltration window and does not reduce regional Strength in the same way.

Alien Facility / Blacksite / UFO missions

  • Alien Facility: evac can be very long. Call early; do not stealth the objective only to spend several dead turns waiting while the command pod finds you. Usually one or two facilities are enough if Golden Path progress is timely.
  • Blacksite: long mission with generous doom-RNF grace. Clear the map, move most of the squad toward evac, then have the fastest soldier take the vial so the RNF phase starts with the team already positioned.
  • Raid UFO: treat it more like a Troop Ambush than a normal timer mission. The post-reveal beacon timer is long enough for controlled clearing; a Specialist can hack the beacon remotely.
  • UFO Avenger Defense: 12 soldiers and many enemies; Defense Matrix turrets are excellent value. Once RNFs begin they are relentless, even though the UI may not warn you clearly.

Chosen Avenger Defense

  • Stable 1.2 is substantially more manageable than the notorious older version, but it is still a 40+ enemy endurance battle plus a Chosen.
  • Defense Matrix turrets with an Engineer are exceptionally valuable. Andy found the small external guns less important than simply killing enemies efficiently.
  • Play slowly; the Avenger power timer is more forgiving than it appears. The mission has no normal reinforcement stream.
  • The cleanest prevention plan is still to reveal/assault the Stronghold before the Chosen completes knowledge progress. Avoid kidnappings and save the “Counter Chosen Activity” covert action for the month it actually buys time.

Retaliations

  • Quick response, no infiltration, short launch window, Chosen present, haven adviser present. Bring the best available squad; SPARKs are especially efficient because infiltration penalties do not matter here.
  • Rebels are expendable relative to trained soldiers. Use rebel grenades early rather than assuming the rebel survives long enough to spend them later.
  • Full Retaliation: continuous RNF begins very early; rescue what you safely can and leave rather than trying to save every rebel.
  • Data Tap: soldiers and rebels start separated; mobility is crucial. SPARK Overdrive, Phase Walk and long-range fire help bridge the map.
  • Supply Convoy: survive fixed RNF waves; regrouping matters more than blindly dashing to the rebels.
  • Recruit Raid: front-loaded RNF waves then relative calm. Smoke/defensible terrain can be unusually valuable here.

SITREP: Project Miranda

  • Extremely warping because the accuracy penalty hurts XCOM’s shooter-heavy turns disproportionately. It becomes more manageable later when Psi, Grenadier, Technical, Assault and SPARK can contribute without relying on normal Aim rolls. Still avoid high-enemy-count Miranda missions unless the reward is worth it.

SITREP: Lost

  • Treat Lost as a serious risk multiplier, not free distraction. They interfere with your movement, explosives, concealment and ammo, and ADVENT is not guaranteed to prioritize them.
  • Technical flamethrowers, pistol perks/Faceoff, Shinobi Bladestorm/Reaper and Gunner area suppression are strong tools.
  • MEC explosives can create a feedback loop by spawning more Lost. Avoid unnecessary explosions, but do not let a Muton Elite or similarly lethal enemy act merely to avoid another horde.
  • Lost Grapplers have Bladestorm. Prioritize Brutes immediately; when the horde is distant, Dashers are often the movement threat, while nearby Grapplers are the immediate melee trap.

8. Tactical operating procedure

Leapfrog movement

Move a rear soldier into the lead, then another rear soldier into the lead. You can often move the squad a full blue move while exposing only a few tiles of new fog. This is especially important on 2-man stealth missions and timed maps.

Flashbang doctrine

  • Casey takes roughly three flashbangs per mission for the first two months.
  • Flash the Sectoid, not the mind-controlled soldier or zombie, to break mind control / dispel the zombie.
  • Flashbangs disable Stun Lancer melee and reduce many enemies to low-threat shooters.
  • Non-Grenadiers are good flash carriers because flashbang throw range is naturally long; let Grenadiers use their range advantage on actual grenades.

RNF timer rule

If RNF is already ticking: take a good first fight quickly. If RNF begins on reveal: move close to the objective before breaking concealment.

Five mission phases

  1. Move in concealment: preserve concealment, watch hidden civilians, give building corners a wide berth, use SMG mobility, and use Specialist hacks to disable a blocking Drone without necessarily breaking concealment.
  2. Ambush pod 1: do not wait for perfection if RNF is active. Flamethrower can be a dominant opener; grenade ambush is fine, but follow with guaranteed damage. Keep a scout concealed when possible.
  3. Move without concealment: slow down. Scouts need deliberate LOS-safe moves. Use sound cues, LOS-breaking terrain and Scanner. Overwatch creeping every turn is too slow.
  4. Fight pod 2 at range: this is often the hardest fight. Rockets/Grenadier cover destruction, Combat Protocol, Electroshock and suppression matter. Do not hoard ordinance if you need it now.
  5. Final pod / unchained movement: once every remaining enemy location is known, melee and aggressive movement become much safer. This is the Assault/Shinobi moment.

1.2 scouting update

  • Scanning Protocol is now Casey's preferred primary information tool on most concealed missions once the Gremlin is upgraded.
  • Do not waste scans before the ambush unless necessary; a forward blue move before scanning increases coverage.
  • Shinobi still scouts long missions, non-concealed starts, stealth ops and situations where a Sharpshooter needs a spotter.
  • Bad scouting is worse than no scouting. Casey repeatedly warns that rushing corners/roofs can end campaigns.

Tanking

Mind-controlled enemies are ideal tanks. If unavailable, Templar is Casey's best conventional tank, with SPARK next. Temporary tanking can be created with Aid Protocol, Untouchable, Null Ward, Mind Merge and similar tools.

Overwatch rules — decide what ruleset you are playing

Casey's beginner recommendation: enable the [WOTC] Revert Overwatch Rules Change mod. Without the revert, single-tile moves can trigger overwatch, merely entering the overwatcher's visibility can trigger it, and the overwatcher receives a substantial aim advantage against units in cover. If you leave the WOTC rules enabled, movement that would be safe in older Long War can draw fire.

Concealment → ambush → second pod → final pod: micro rules

While concealed

  • Assume civilians may be hidden behind high-cover objects at the insertion area.
  • Give building corners and other LOS blockers a wide berth.
  • SMG users can fan out a little while concealed to help find the first pod.
  • A Specialist can disable/hack a Drone without necessarily breaking squad concealment.
  • If sneaking around pods, the far map edge is often the safest route.

First ambush

  • If RNF is already ticking, take a good enough ambush instead of wasting turns.
  • If RNF has not started, get close to the objective first.
  • Preserve the Shinobi/Reaper scout when possible.
  • Do not risk soldiers for loot; guaranteed grenade/Combat Protocol damage is worth using to close the fight.

Moving revealed

  • Yellow-alert enemies can sometimes shoot on activation; avoid exposing flanks to a pod drawn by noise.
  • Rooftops can be excellent Shinobi scouting positions when the approach is safe.
  • If the RNF warning turns red, hide the concealed Shinobi before the drop and then move/retreat quickly.
  • When forced to double-move blind, move a lower-ranked soldier first into strong cover.
  • Move two-action fighters such as Sharpshooter and rocket Technical only after safer scouts/movers have shown the path.

Second pod at range

  • Do not hoard rockets/grenades for a hypothetical later battle; this is often the hardest engagement.
  • Electroshock is usually preferable to charging an Assault into unknown fog.
  • A Grenadier + Specialist can create guaranteed long-range damage/cover destruction even when ordinary shots are poor.

1.2 edge-case warnings

  • Enemy pods can patrol roughly 8 tiles in a straight normal move; do not assume a pod is safely out of reach because it looked distant last turn.
  • Purifier flamethrower is not disabled by Flashbang.
  • Berserker and Archon melee are not disabled by burning when they can still reach a target.
  • Stunning a Spectre does not release an already-Shadowbound soldier.
  • Suppression is not a perfect answer to ADVENT Rocketeers; they can sometimes run it and still launch a rocket.
  • Late-game ADVENT can gain Covering Fire; movement/action sequencing around overwatch becomes more dangerous.

Andy’s probability / consistency doctrine v6

ANDY A 90% shot is not “guaranteed”; across hundreds of shots, misses are inevitable. Tactical quality means arranging turns so a single miss does not decide the squad’s fate. Distinguish bad outcome from a good decision from bad outcome caused by a bad decision.

Equipment / movement micro

  • Do not sleep on plating. Ablative HP prevents both deaths and long wound timers.
  • An empty utility slot can be treated as an intentional +1 Mobility item.
  • For Andy’s control builds, SMGs commonly go to Shinobi, Technical, Grenadier and most Specialists; mobility matters more than rifle damage they rarely use.
  • Early AP Rounds are extremely valuable on primary shooters.
  • Once Skullmining exists, a Specialist’s Skulljack is also a hack-stat tool, not only a story item.
  • Hacking a robot does not break concealment.
  • Stunning an enemy removes its detection radius, which can rescue a concealed Shinobi caught too close.

Two-person stealth hack — Andy’s execution

  1. Use Shinobi + Specialist, usually with SMGs and minimal weight.
  2. Scout a safe line-of-sight tile to the objective.
  3. Throw evac before committing; this may reveal the Specialist, so the Shinobi must keep the route safe.
  4. Hack and, if needed, use Command/Teamwork to pull the Specialist back out.
  5. A smoke grenade can buy a desperate turn, but if the pair is revealed before evac setup, survival odds collapse.
Do not generalize stealth hacks to revealed Extract VIP missions. Wandering patrols and overwatch make a lone-scout extraction much less reliable.

Hack rewards / failure reminders

Andy tracks high-impact hack effects such as all-robot control, temporary enemy control, disorient, action-point refund, concealment, targeting/crit buffs and supply/Intel caches. Failure effects can include Map Alert, Reinforcements or enemy stat buffs. Map Alert is not always disastrous if you are already in a defensive position and prefer enemies to come to you.

Complete merged tactical reference — probability, movement, stealth, control, hacking, loadouts & edge cases

Probability discipline

  • Long War is a campaign of hundreds of rolls. A 90–99% shot will eventually miss. The tactical objective is not to eliminate variance; it is to avoid creating turns where a single roll decides the squad’s survival.
  • Separate bad luck from bad planning. A strong decision can fail; repeated “unlucky” disasters usually reveal a positioning, scouting, activation or backup-plan problem.
  • Prefer guaranteed damage/control and high-probability actions when the consequence of failure is catastrophic. Save speculative shots for turns where a miss is survivable.

Movement and pod control

  • The governing tactical rule remains fight one pod at a time. Close-range classes tempt you to dash/Fleche/Run & Gun into unrevealed tiles; do not do that unless you are prepared for another pod.
  • Leapfrog movement: move rear soldiers into the lead rather than repeatedly pushing the current lead farther into fog. You preserve speed while exposing fewer new tiles each action.
  • Enemy patrols can cover substantial distance on their turn. When a scout is close to an unseen pod, leave enough space for patrol movement rather than assuming the pod will remain stationary.
  • On fully infiltrated missions, do not let fear of reinforcements force reckless movement. A single RNF drop is easier than fighting two natural pods simultaneously.

Early control and flashbang rules

  • Early flashbangs are campaign-saving utility. They break Sectoid Mind Control/Zombies when the Sectoid is targeted, disable Stun Lancer melee, reduce aim/mobility and combine well with Suppression.
  • Not every enemy ability respects flashbang/disorientation: Purifier flamethrowers remain usable, and some late enemies resist or ignore particular control effects.
  • Control evolves over the campaign: early Flashbang/Suppression/Hack/Arc Thrower/Flamethrower → midgame Combatives/Incendiary/Frost/Insanity → late Stasis/Domination/Sting/Dragon/Disabling Shot/Full Override/Chain Lightning.
  • By summer, a large share of the barracks should contribute some form of reliable control. The point is to avoid turns where every dangerous enemy must be killed outright.

Loadout doctrine

  • Plating is not optional fluff. Ablative HP often converts a lethal hit into survival and also reduces wound duration. Even late, Andy commonly dedicates multiple inventory slots to plating/vest protection.
  • An empty slot is an item if the extra Mobility is strategically valuable. Technicals/Shinobis and stealth teams often benefit more from movement than from a marginal grenade or vest.
  • SMGs belong on classes whose primary job is not directed fire: Technical, Grenadier, Shinobi and usually Specialist. Ranger is the main conventional rifle user in Andy’s doctrine; Psi uses SMG until Phase Walk, then can exploit a shotgun.
  • AP rounds are excellent early on, particularly for Rangers/Sharpshooters against armored mechanicals. Laser sights do not use vanilla distance falloff, so crit Sharpshooters/Assaults can use them without the usual short-range assumption.
  • Do not waste autoloader charges before a Codex Psionic Bomb or other forced reload situation if you can avoid it.

Mechanical hacking package

  • Hacking gets dramatically better when supported. Stack Skulljack/Skullmining Hack bonus + Redscreen + Bluescreen Bombs to move higher-tier MECs into reliable control range.
  • Andy’s test case found Redscreen slightly stronger than Bluescreen Bombs for control chance, but both together are the real breakpoint. Because hacking a disoriented robot clears disorientation, sequence your control actions intentionally.
  • Permanent/temporary controlled robots also absorb enemy fire, creating defensive value beyond their direct attacks.
  • Specialist hacks and Combat Protocol can sometimes be executed via long straight-line line of sight even when the Specialist is not near the fight; search backward as well as forward for valid tiles.
  • Hacking a robot does not break concealment.

Stealth-hack missions

  • Typical team: concealed Shinobi/officer + Specialist or similarly mobile objective unit. Bring SMGs, minimal equipment for Mobility, one Smoke if possible, and a bonded pair when available.
  • Scout to a safe tile with line of sight to the objective, call evac early, then keep the hacker concealed until the evac timer is nearly complete. Command/Teamwork can pull the hacker back after an objective action.
  • If detected before the evac flare, survival odds collapse. If detected after flare, Smoke and control may buy enough time.
  • Spider Suit grapple movement does not break concealment, though Andy often skips Spider Suit research for broader economy reasons.
  • Do not try the same trick on every revealed VIP extraction; patrol behavior and overwatch can make an apparently simple Shinobi escort much more dangerous than a standard stealth hack.
  • Sewer/abandoned maps can make tiny stealth squads unusually dangerous; aborting a bad map is a valid strategic choice.

Hack rewards and failure penalties

  • Hack rewards include supply/intel caches, temporary squad buffs (Aim/Crit/Dodge/Squadsight/Run & Gun/concealment/vision), Facility Leads and other tactical effects. Large early Supply rewards can be a meaningful economy swing.
  • Failure penalties can include Reinforcements, Map Alert and major enemy stat buffs. Do not chase a marginal bonus if the failure result is campaign-threatening.
  • Some reward categories do not stack the way the interface may imply; always judge the actual reward rather than assuming two identical bonuses necessarily double.

Graze, Overwatch and status edge cases

  • Graze is not simply a miss. On multi-shot/shred/special-ammo classes it can still trigger useful secondary effects, which is why some authors value Grazing Fire more on Gunners than on pure finisher classes.
  • Overwatch rules depend on whether the Revert Overwatch Rules mod is enabled. Without the revert, one-tile moves and entering visibility can trigger reaction fire, and cover is less protective against Overwatch.
  • Stunning a Spectre does not automatically release Shadowbind; Revival Protocol can wake the bound soldier while the clone persists.
  • Disorient disables Andromedon Acid Bomb, but Burning does not stop every melee attack: Berserkers/Archons and Muton counter behavior have important exceptions.
  • Suppression is strong but not absolute; certain Rocketeers can run it and still produce a dangerous rocket. Late ADVENT Covering Fire also makes careless movement far more punishing.
  • Incendiaries interact badly with water tiles: do not rely on fire effects on targets standing in rivers/fountains.

Explosives, corpses and loot

  • Full-salvage missions make corpse preservation strategically important. Avoid explosive finishing blows on enemies whose corpses you specifically need when another safe kill exists.
  • Early Sectoids are especially valuable potential Elerium Core carriers. If you need Cores for the opening economy, do not casually destroy them with explosives.
  • Do not hoard ordinance through a crisis. If three pods activate together, spend the rockets/grenades/incendiaries now; a healthy team with empty consumables is better than a wounded team that “saved” resources.
Small mechanics worth preserving: Andy records early hack rewards of roughly 35 Supplies for a small cache and 75 for a large cache, making early objective hacks economically meaningful. He also notes a pistol UI bug where the interface can display a movement penalty even when the perk that removes it is functioning correctly; trust the actual Mobility result rather than the misleading display.

Progress UI and mission availability

  • Resistance Management dots: 0 = no progress, 1 = Lib 1 complete, 2 = Lib 2 complete. There is no third dot; after Lib 3 the Network Tower appears.
  • The dot/status may not refresh until you return to the strategy map.
  • Once unlocked, Network Tower and HQ do not expire. Intel pressure is mainly about finding Lib 1–3 with enough time.

Lib 2 — detection and tempo

Casey's classic Legend target is roughly 5+ days of detection time before committing a real squad; he was willing to use a 30-Intel boost on this campaign-critical step. Treat those figures as Casey's difficulty/version benchmark, not a universal fixed 1.2 price.

Lib 2 effectively behaves as though regional Strength were about +1 for reinforcement pressure. That makes a weak timer worse than it looks. Waiting for a better detection roll is often cheaper than forcing a bad one.

Lib 3 — +2 effective Strength and finesse variants

  • Lib 3 behaves at roughly +2 regional Strength for RNF pressure in Casey's framework.
  • Destroy Relay: sneak almost all the way to the objective before breaking concealment.
  • Neutralize VIP: RNF begins immediately; Hidden Faceless density can make the extraction zone much nastier than the visible enemy count suggests.
  • A long-range assassination/finesse plan can be safer than clearing the map: Sharpshooter shots, vehicle explosions and rooftop Death From Above can solve the VIP while the squad avoids unnecessary pods.

Network Tower — exact setup

  1. Run it soon after revealing it; ADVENT Strength can continue climbing.
  2. Casey likes extra infiltration when it meaningfully lowers enemy activity/detection radius, but in LWOTC he is less willing to burn Intel just to push an already-manageable Tower from Light-Moderate to Light.
  3. A Ghostwalker + Covert Shinobi remains uniquely good because this mission is about physically slipping past pods, not merely knowing where they are.
  4. Spider Suit is a specific escape trick: Grapple can pull the Shinobi out of a detection trap without alerting the pod.
  5. When the Shinobi can reach the console in one turn, reposition the other four soldiers—ideally toward the Command Center.
  6. Triggering the console creates a two-turn enemy stun window. Use it to erase the Command Pod and nearby pods before they recover.
  7. The Assault is especially good at immediately deleting the Command Pod once the stun lands.

ADVENT HQ — endurance doctrine

  • Expect roughly 40+ enemies; Casey recommends a full LWOTC team around eight soldiers for a first/serious attempt.
  • Bring many Medkits and expect to use them. EXO suits/extra ordnance are strong, but preserve at least one serious control charge for the General.
  • Find a strong defensive roof/position and let multiple pods come to you rather than advancing after every kill.
  • Avoid pulling double-Drone pods or the Command Pod into the main firefight if you can help it.
  • Only hunt the Command Pod after the rest of the map is nearly cleared.

General control cheat sheet

Assault Stun · Flamethrower · Incendiary · Stasis · Sting. Casey's cleanest loop is Stun → Stasis → Stun, exploiting Stun's one-turn cooldown. An aggressive alternative is Shred → Rapid Fire Assault → Officer Command.

Invasion — timing and preparation

  • An Invasion cannot occur until at least 21 days after Liberation.
  • It also requires a neighboring region around Strength 8+; a growing adjacent strength stack is your warning that an invasion force may be forming.
  • Once the 21-day window opens, put a strong, equipped soldier in the liberated haven as advisor.
  • Primary beacon plan: Stealth Shinobi gets vision while Sharpshooter destroys the beacon. Roofed beacons can sometimes be solved by destroying the support/roof or by long-range rifle fire.
  • Fallbacks include rockets/Bombard, a stealth melee approach, or brute-force mobile killers.
  • LWOTC version is milder than old LW2; Casey sometimes recommends delaying the final beacon kill long enough to farm a few safe reinforcement drops/corpses, then ending it before the battle becomes unstable.

Andy’s Network Tower execution v6

  • Andy treats Tower as the one mission that is effectively stealth-mandatory: only five soldiers and aggressive post-reveal RNF.
  • Move the Shinobi decisively; excessive dawdling creates more patrol risk. Sergeant/Covert is valuable, Ghostwalker Shinobi is an ideal specialist if you happen to have one.
  • Approach the final building from the right (right while facing it from spawn): Andy notes the left side can contain a turret while the right side does not in his observed layouts.
  • Stage combat troops so each is within a blue move of a pod before hacking. The stun window is wasted if everyone must spend it running.
  • Trigger the computer on the first action of the turn whenever possible.
  • Andy describes the stun as effectively giving three XCOM turns of advantage: activation turn, following stunned turn, then the enemy’s wake-up turn without normal action before XCOM acts again.
  • Codices and Sustaining Priests can behave as exceptions; plan them as priority cleanup.
  • Do not put SMGs on the entire team just to sneak; after the hack you still must kill the map.

Complete merged Liberation mechanics & tactical playbook

Strategic sequence and detection

  • First Liberation is a major campaign objective and gets harder as time/Strength rise. Legend suppresses Liberation mission appearance very early in the campaign, but once available there is always a current chain mission in each contacted region; the challenge is detection.
  • Progress display: 0 dots → no progress; 1 → Lib1 complete; 2 → Lib2 complete; Lib3 does not show as a third dot because the Network Tower appears instead. The screen may not refresh until returning to geoscape after completing a step.
  • Lib1: hack-for-Intel mission, strong stealth candidate.
  • Lib2: Rescue/Extract Defector; roughly +1 Strength relative to a normal mission and much harder to detect. Put the haven on Intel, scan with Avenger, and boost when necessary to fit a real squad.
  • Lib3: Neutralize VIP or Destroy Relay/Sabotage-style mission, roughly +2 Strength. It is the most dangerous detection/infiltration gate; Faceless civilians can be a major complication. Taking side missions while fishing for Lib3 can spike Vigilance/Strength and sabotage the chain.
  • After Lib3, Network Tower/HQ no longer expire like detected guerrilla ops, but Strength continues rising, so do not postpone them indefinitely.

Network Tower — stealth setup and three-turn payoff

  • Five-soldier cap and very aggressive RNF once revealed. This is the Liberation mission most dependent on stealthing the hack.
  • If the computer is hacked before concealment breaks, map enemies are stunned without scattering. That creates an enormous opening for grenades/AoE against intact pods.
  • 150% infiltration can reduce enemy count; going beyond can improve detection radius but often ties up the squad too long to be worth chasing.
  • Move with purpose. Andy’s lesson from DerAva is that excessive dawdling gives patrols more opportunities to close off routes. Fast does not mean blind—advance as far as safely scouted each turn.
  • Shinobi is the natural infiltrator; Ghostwalker/Covert are bonuses but not mandatory. Reaper is viable if damage output is sufficient (Banish materially helps). Specialist can be awkward because the terminal cannot simply be remote-hacked from outside and their mop-up damage is weak.
  • Do not force the whole squad to follow the hacker. Stage the other four near pods/command-room approaches so they can exploit the stun immediately.
  • Enemies effectively lose multiple actions after the hack. To maximize that window, hack with the infiltrator’s first action, not last.
  • Extra actions—Command/Teamwork/Grapple/Phase Walk—should support the infiltrator. A bonded pair can be more useful than a nominally “perfect” class mix.
  • Patrols can move roughly eight tiles; include that potential movement in your detection-radius planning.
  • Map-specific heuristics from Andy: the right side of the final building is generally safer from the left-side turret spawn; side bridge ledges are often useful hiding lanes; a pod on the roof can imply the command room itself is emptier.
  • Enemy exceptions: Priest Sustain can break the expected stun flow; Codex can clear stun and act sooner than expected. Do not assume every enemy stays inert for the full same duration.
  • Mop-up squad ideas: Shinobi + strong shooters/control (Ranger/Assault/Grenadier/Skirmisher); SPARK can be excellent; Psi becomes viable when Phase Walk + real offensive abilities are online; early Technical can solve clustered pods.

HQ Assault

  • Huge full-salvage map with many enemies and one of the best corpse hauls in the campaign. Large map size makes one-pod-at-a-time play possible if you stay disciplined.
  • HQ removes up to a large chunk of regional Strength; doing it at high enough Strength maximizes strategic reduction, but do not delay the first Liberation merely to optimize that number.
  • Ten soldiers takes extremely long to infiltrate. Andy commonly aims for ~8 strong soldiers, sometimes nine, and often boosts. Use A-team gear; this mission competes heavily with all other infiltrations.
  • Claim defensible terrain early and let pods come to you. Do not let Assault/Shinobi “easy button” attacks drag the squad forward into additional pods. Keep the scout concealed until the General pod if possible.
  • Turrets can accidentally join fights; identify/disable them before activating nearby pods. Specialist/Trojan is valuable even on a healer-oriented soldier.
  • Repeatable cooldown abilities outperform consumables over the whole mission, but when multiple pods converge, spend every grenade/rocket needed to regain control. The correct resource metric is surviving the critical turn.
  • Sharpshooters/Reapers are excellent; two snipers can be reasonable. Extended Mag beats Auto-loader because the mission gives you time to reload/regroup between engagements.
  • Pause between fights to heal, move rear soldiers forward and reset cooldowns. Doom RNF is so late that patient play is rewarded.
  • Completing HQ can immediately boost another active infiltration in the region; this rare interaction can be exploited deliberately if another mission is waiting.

Invasion

  • Can occur starting about 21 days after Liberation and requires ADVENT staging from a nearby high-Strength region; a sudden nearby Strength spike is a warning.
  • LWOTC Invasion is much more manageable than old LW2, but it is still an A-team quick-response mission with a Chosen.
  • Keep moving toward the beacon. A short grace period becomes frequent RNFs; once every-turn drops begin, spending multiple turns cleaning every pod is a losing plan.
  • Kill the Chosen early when possible, then destroy the beacon. After the beacon falls, only the final scheduled RNF remains and the mission ends after the remaining threat is resolved; you do not need to shepherd every rebel back to evac.
  • Andy generally does not hunt the precursor mission because it would require Intel workers in a liberated haven that could instead generate Supplies, and the full Invasion itself yields valuable corpses.
Liberation detection/refresh details: after completing a liberation mission, the Resistance Management dots may not update until you return to the strategy map and reopen the screen; no extra scan is required. Lib missions respawn after expiry, and in current LWOTC repeated Lib 3 discovery becomes easier rather than relying on the old LW2 instant-rediscovery bug. Once Lib 3 is complete, the Network Tower and then HQ are persistent and do not require further Intel detection.
