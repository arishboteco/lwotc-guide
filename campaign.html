1. The rules that keep campaigns alive

1 pod
Fight one pod at a time.

Most catastrophic turns begin when a melee move, flank, roof climb or careless scout activates another pod.

Scan first
Information is combat power.

In 1.2, Specialist Scanning Protocol changes campaign tempo. Use it after the first ambush to find remaining pods and protect flanks.

Control
Do not require every enemy to die this turn.

Burn, stun, hack, Stasis, Suppression and Sting Grenades convert impossible damage checks into manageable fights.

Deep barracks
Build more than one A-team.

Infiltration, wounds, covert actions, officer training and bond training all remove soldiers from availability.

Mission choice
You are not meant to run every mission.

Bad infiltration, high Strength and low-value rewards are valid reasons to pass.

Intel reserve
Expansion beats convenience.

Boost critical missions; do not routinely spend the Intel needed to open regions and reach Golden Path objectives.

Andy setup / mechanics notes worth knowing

  • Do not use Avenger-reskin mods in Andy’s setup; he reports they can break the game.
  • Resistance Orders are disabled by default in LWOTC stable 1.2.x.
  • Lost do not use vanilla Headshot chaining.
  • Training Center AP can buy eligible XCOM-row abilities beyond the soldier’s current rank.
  • Hero classes can bank a promotion by not selecting a perk immediately.
  • Laser sights in LWOTC increase crit chance without the vanilla distance falloff assumption Andy warns about.

Six systems to understand before a serious campaign

1. Missions must be discovered

Most opportunities already exist in the region; your haven has to detect them. Rebels on Intel generate detection, Avenger scanning is worth roughly 4 basic Intel workers, and a Scientist adviser adds a 20% haven-wide Intel bonus. More detection usually means you find missions earlier and get longer infiltration windows.

Haven & expansion details →

2. Strength ≠ Force ≠ Vigilance

Strength is regional ADVENT troop quantity and drives mission enemy counts. Force is global enemy quality/tech progression. Vigilance is ADVENT's alarm level caused mainly by XCOM activity; when Vigilance outruns Strength, ADVENT tends to move more Strength into the region.

Regional operating rules →

3. 100% infiltration removes major penalties

Below 100%, you can face more dangerous reinforcement queues, larger detection radii, slower evac and other mission pressure. Above 100% can improve several of those factors. Andy's beginner rule is sound: plan around 100% and skip missions that cannot justify under-infiltration.

Mission decision tool →

4. Call evac before you need it

The Skyranger is not immediate. Base evac delay is difficulty-dependent, and low infiltration / large squads can add turns. Throwing the flare breaks concealment; moving an existing evac zone adds arrival time. Once evac has arrived, it waits for you.

Evac & reinforcement rules →

5. Corpses are not normally recovered

Most guerrilla missions end by evacuation, so bodies stay behind. Look for Full Salvage missions such as Supply Raids, Troop Maneuvers/Columns, HQ assaults, landed UFOs and invasions when an autopsy or Proving Ground project needs a corpse.

Autopsy priorities →

6. You are expected to decline missions

A dark event or mediocre reward is often cheaper than a squad wipe. Andy's onboarding advice is to prefer missions that can infiltrate a useful 5+ soldier team, cancel an infiltration when a more important mission appears, and reserve Intel boosts mainly for campaign-critical or unusually valuable missions.

Complete new-player onboarding, difficulty & resource guidance
  • LWOTC is a poor first exposure to XCOM 2. Learn the base game and ideally War of the Chosen first; otherwise you are learning vanilla systems, WOTC systems and Long War systems simultaneously.
  • Veteran is the recommended first serious difficulty even for players who have beaten vanilla Legend. Rookie is valid, but be aware that tactics which work there may teach habits that fail on higher difficulties.
  • Honestman over Ironman: preserve the consequence of tactical decisions, but keep multiple normal saves because a heavily modded campaign can suffer bugs/corruption. Reload for technical failures, not to erase ordinary mistakes.
  • Expect the first campaign to be educational rather than pristine. A throw-away campaign to learn infiltration, havens and mission selection is completely reasonable.
  • Infiltration: most missions require squads to spend days infiltrating; launching early generally increases enemy pressure. Plan for 100%, and accept that many missions should simply be skipped.
  • Mission choice: compare reward/penalty, enemy count, Strength, infiltration window and roster conflict. A Dark Event is often survivable; a squad wipe is much harder to recover from. Cancel an existing infiltration if a substantially more important mission appears.
  • Haven default: unliberated havens mostly Intel; Hide when cooling a hot region; Supplies in liberated regions. Keep a soldier adviser and rotate them out instantly when needed for a mission.
  • Haven advisers do not recover Will while assigned. Prefer the highest-rank healthy/well-rested soldier you can spare because rank helps recruiting/Faceless detection and the adviser appears on retaliation missions.
  • Evac: call the Skyranger several turns before you need it. The flare breaks concealment, but once the Skyranger arrives it waits indefinitely; there is no reward for calling it late.
  • Liberation: begin the first chain early. New LWOTC UI labels the missions more clearly than old LW2, but the chain can still be missed if you do not prioritize its detection.

Research speed — turn RP into calendar days

LWOTC research is linear: Tygan contributes 10 RP/hour; each normal, research-available Scientist contributes 5 RP/hour; a Scientist staffed in the Laboratory contributes 10 RP/hour; Scientists staffed elsewhere contribute no normal research output. Each completed Basic Research adds another 5 RP/hour.

Base research days = RP cost ÷ [(10 + 5×unassigned Scientists + 10×Lab Scientists + 5×Basic Research completions) × 24]

How to acquire the bottleneck resources

Corpses & wrecks

Most evac missions do not recover bodies. Prioritize full-salvage missions such as Troop Maneuvers / troop-column ambushes, Supply Line Raids, regional HQ assaults and other full-salvage fights when you need a specific autopsy corpse. Explosive killing blows can destroy recoverable materiel, so preserve key targets when practical.

Alloys / Elerium / Cores

Full-salvage missions are the cleanest source. Smash N Grab resource caches can also yield Supplies, Alloys, Elerium or Cores even though they do not return corpses. Use the Black Market to bridge a breakpoint, not to erase every shortage.

Intel

Keep enough for Resistance Communications/Radio and Golden Path research before spending it on routine mission boosts. Save datapads/caches when a large contact or Shadow Chamber bill is approaching.

Research capacity

A Scientist produces value every day you acquire them early. Once you can support a mature Lab, lab staffing doubles each staffed Scientist’s research contribution from 5 to 10 RP/hour. Do not over-tech so far ahead that you cannot afford the equipment you just unlocked.

Decision rule when you miss a monthly threshold: protect the next weapon/armor breakpoint, then the Golden Path requirement, then convenience research. A delayed Purifier/Viper/Faceless autopsy is usually recoverable; entering a new force-level band while a full weapon tier behind is much harder to recover from.

Complete merged research & Proving Ground rationale — not just the sequence

Opening research logic

  • Resistance Communications first in the Psi-oriented opening because a second region is a strategic requirement, not a convenience.
  • Modular Weapons is a short detour that unlocks early weapon upgrades; scopes/stocks have outsized value before soldier aim and weapon tiers mature.
  • Alien Biotech → Sectoid Autopsy → Psionics is the core Psi branch. Psionics itself has a Scientist gate; the point of starting early is that advanced Psi training consumes a large amount of calendar time.
  • Trooper Autopsy / Hybrid Materials are the survivability branch. Casey explicitly accepts moving them earlier if the campaign is taking too many wounds, because plating/vest HP can convert deaths into light wounds and reduce recovery time.
  • Laser → Advanced Laser is the first major damage breakpoint. Corpse-dependent interrupts such as Robotics, Purifier or Stun Lancer should jump ahead when the required bodies are actually available.
  • Andy delays an autopsy if performing it would consume the last corpses needed for the resulting item/project. The guide therefore treats corpse count as part of the research condition, not merely a checkbox that the autopsy exists.

Mid/late research logic

  • After Lasers, research becomes less linear. Weapons remain the priority; armor is important, but both authors generally prefer the next weapon breakpoint before optional defensive/convenience technology.
  • Advanced Magnetic, Combat Armor and Battle Armor form the core midgame path; Resistance Radio is delayed as long as geography allows because its Intel cost competes with expansion/Golden Path spending.
  • Elerium → Coilguns → Advanced Coilguns is the next major offensive breakpoint. Check resource prices before spending Alloys/Elerium/Cores on optional projects.
  • Alien Encryption, Codex Brain, Blacksite Vial and Psionic Gate are campaign-path technologies. Do not let optional research stall them once the roster can handle story missions.
  • Powered Armor then Plasma branches complete the late combat curve. Mobile/Spider/Shadow armor is a late luxury in Andy’s route and can be skipped if the campaign is already nearing completion.
  • Andy explicitly considers Basic Research / Basic Engineering poor use of research time in his 1.2.2 route.

Proving Ground operating rule

  • The PG does not need to be built immediately. Late April is a reasonable timing because the early campaign often lacks both unlocks and spare Engineers.
  • Engineer staffing is dynamic: accelerate a project when the breakpoint matters, but do not permanently trap scarce Engineers in the PG while excavation/power/facilities are bottlenecked.
  • Andy’s old 1.2.2 route strongly prioritized early SPARKs. Stable 1.2.3 reworked SPARKs, so the timing concept is retained but the old exact SPARK valuation is marked version-sensitive.

High-value PG projects

  • Alloy Plating; Incendiary Grenades; Skulljack/Skullmining; Talon/Dragon/Shredder/Bluescreen/Redscreen ammunition; Plasma Grenades; upgraded heavy weapons; advanced grenades; selected alien armors; Battlefield Medicine/Nanomedikit; tactical plating/vests when they solve a specific problem.
  • Incendiaries are repeatedly singled out because burning is one of the most reliable forms of control against organic enemies.
  • Redscreen + Bluescreen effects are strategically important because they make higher-tier mechanical hacks practical, not merely because they do damage.

Projects commonly skipped or delayed

  • Specialty ammo with narrow use cases (Needle/Flechette/Stiletto), low-priority vests (Hellweave/Chameleon/Stasis), most non-incendiary grenade projects, Mimic Beacons, and routine weapon-mod upgrade projects.
  • Plasma Blaster is situational: useful if Shredstorm is far away; less compelling once the stronger heavy-weapon option is available. Casey’s 1.2 note also values the extra ammo count versus Shredder Gun, so this is a legitimate source disagreement rather than a universal skip.
  • Do not research every alien-hunter weapon early. Andy mainly values the Frost Bomb from that package before the PG economy is comfortable.
Basic Research / Engineering: Andy considers these poor uses of time in his normal 1.2.2 route, and Casey explicitly backed away from an early Basic Research rush. Treat them as gap-fillers when normal research is temporarily blocked, not default opening priorities.

March–early April: survival and compounding value

4. Regions, havens and expansion

Starting region

  • Start with essentially everyone on Intel.
  • Assign a Haven Advisor immediately; Casey likes Ranger or Assault early because they handle Faceless well.
  • His early routine: scan the starting region until you have found roughly two useful missions, then use Black Market scanning time.
  • Jailbreaks usually provide enough new rebels; Recruiting is situational rather than a permanent job.
  • When rebels change jobs they lose roughly a day of productivity, so do not constantly shuffle Intel ↔ Supply.

When to open the first new region

Practical trigger: contact an adjacent region as soon as Resistance Communications and your Intel budget allow — ideally while the starting region is still a productive low-Strength mission farm, not after it is already at Strength 5.

Casey's explicit rule is to expand quickly. Your starting region accumulates Vigilance because you are doing missions there. ADVENT responds by increasing Strength. You want the next low-Strength region operational before standard missions in the first become unattractive.

Strength 5+ treatment

  • At about Strength 5, Supply gathering becomes a reasonable alternative because normal missions become harder to execute.
  • Intel is still useful in hot regions for Troop Ambushes and stealthable extraction/hack opportunities.
  • Casey often hunts a Troop Ambush, then switches the haven to Supplies for a couple of weeks, then returns to Intel when another ambush can plausibly spawn.
  • He tracks Troop Ambush timing: roughly a 21-day cooldown from spawn; his 1.2 notes simplify the practical post-expiry window to roughly 9–12 days + remaining time on the current ambush.
  • Only two Troop Ambushes can be active globally at once in the older framework he describes.

Liberation region

  • Once you choose a liberation region, put a large share — often all — rebels on Intel when hunting Lib 2/3.
  • Do not run every unrelated mission that appears there. Extra activity raises Vigilance/Strength and makes the liberation missions harder.
  • Lib 2 is hard to detect; Casey's classic approach is to let bad-timer versions expire and keep searching for a version with enough time to send a full team with an Intel boost.

After first liberation

  • First liberation target: approximately July.
  • Once HQ is beaten and the Blacksite is revealed, start expanding toward it immediately.
  • Ignore continent bonuses unless they are conveniently on the Golden Path route.
  • Expect the Blacksite route to be expensive. Casey gives an example path costing about 500 Intel plus a 350-supply relay.
  • After Blacksite, repeat the same logic for Forge and Psi Gate regions.
  • Resistance Communications can often be delayed because covert actions and points of interest can provide contacts.

Early scanning & Troop Ambush timing — exact notes

  • Start with essentially all rebels on Intel, assign a Haven Advisor, and scan the starting region until roughly two missions are found; then spend scan time at the Black Market.
  • Changing a rebel's job causes roughly 24 hours of lost productivity; avoid constant Intel ↔ Supply toggling.
  • Older Casey timing: Troop Ambushes have a 21-day cooldown from spawn and only two can be active globally.
  • 1.2 practical estimate: once an Ambush expires, another can become eligible in roughly 9–12 days. While one is active, estimate next eligibility as 9–12 days + current mission time remaining.
  • At high Strength Casey often keeps Intel long enough to find the Ambush, then flips to Supplies for a couple of weeks before searching again.

Complete merged strategy-layer reference — region stats, haven jobs, economy, expansion & campaign cadence
Completeness rule: this layer preserves the practical exceptions and operating routines from Andy and Casey instead of reducing them to one-line rules. Current stable mechanics still override older strategy advice when numbers conflict.

Region state: Strength, Vigilance and when to leave

  • Strength is the regional ADVENT presence that drives mission enemy counts and reinforcement pressure. Routine missions become much less attractive around Strength 5+, and both authors increasingly shift activity to other regions rather than trying to brute-force the same haven indefinitely.
  • Vigilance is ADVENT's alarm level. When Vigilance is above Strength, troop movement into the region becomes more likely. Andy notes Vigilance decays on a multi-day timer, and more active rebels can lengthen that decay; completing another mission in the region resets the decay cycle.
  • Andy’s practical high-Strength rule is stricter than Casey’s older one: at Strength 5+, normal operations should largely stop; at Strength 8, avoid ordinary missions entirely because Snare-style reinforcement behavior can turn normal-looking hack missions into traps.
  • There is one recurring exception: Troop Ambush / Troop Column missions remain attractive because they reduce regional Strength and provide full salvage. Both authors accept continuing to search for them after normal missions have become unattractive.
  • Strength can rise while a squad is infiltrating. If Vigilance has spiked in a low-Strength region, assume the mission may launch one enemy-count bracket harder than it looked when you started infiltration.

Haven jobs and retaliation management

  • When actively farming a region, default to Intel. More detection means earlier mission discovery and longer infiltration windows. Avenger scanning and a Scientist adviser improve detection further.
  • Casey’s early routine is to assign a soldier adviser immediately, scan the region until viable missions are found, then use spare scanning time elsewhere rather than sitting at the Black Market from day one.
  • Andy increasingly avoids the Recruit job. Jailbreaks are usually a more efficient source of soldiers/rebels, and Intel workers are what reveal Jailbreaks. One exception is a nearly empty haven where very few Intel workers would only discover unusably short-timer missions.
  • When a region is deliberately cooling down, switch most rebels to Hide. Andy notes that up to roughly two non-hiding workers can often be left active without creating the same retaliation pressure; he commonly leaves a small Intel presence to look for a Troop Ambush.
  • In liberated regions, run Supplies and staff an Engineer adviser for income. Keep or rotate a soldier adviser long enough to find/remove hidden Faceless. Unexpectedly reduced supply income is a warning that a Faceless may still be present.
  • Job changes impose a temporary productivity penalty, so do not bounce rebels between Intel/Supplies/Recruit every few hours just to micromanage a small gain.
  • Andy’s retaliation heuristics: several rebels on Intel/Supplies/Recruit can cross job-specific retaliation thresholds. The exact prediction system is more detailed than a single threshold, so use the current Haven Management screen/UFOpaedia when timing matters.
  • Recruited rebels have a non-trivial chance to be Faceless. Certain advisers/scanning tools reduce that risk; higher-rank soldiers and officers also improve adviser performance.

Expansion and mission throughput

  • Expand before you feel forced to. If every discovered mission has a poor timer, high enemy count or bad Strength, the strategic failure usually happened earlier: you did not have enough contacted low-Strength regions generating missions.
  • Andy targets roughly nine missions per supply-drop cycle, which implies several squads infiltrating simultaneously and at least two productive low-Strength regions. This is a campaign-health benchmark, not a hard quota.
  • Most routine missions should use around five soldiers. Six is often a luxury or a chance to bring a lower-rank soldier. Seven on ordinary guerrilla ops is usually strategically expensive because infiltration time ties up too much of the roster; reserve large squads for genuinely large/untimed missions.
  • By April, if a mission has a forgiving enemy count and a strong core team, deliberately bring a Squaddie or other lower-rank soldier. Deep roster development matters more than perfect A-team XP concentration.
  • A healthy campaign eventually needs enough depth to keep two to three combat squads active while still supporting covert ops, officer training, bond training, wounds and haven advisers.

Intel discipline and boosting

  • Routine Intel boosting is usually a symptom of insufficient expansion. Intel is the currency for contacting regions, critical liberation/Golden Path boosts and story research.
  • Andy’s regular boost exceptions are Troop Ambushes, Supply Raids, Liberation missions and Golden Path missions, and even there he prefers not to boost if the timer is already adequate.
  • Casey similarly recommends stockpiling Intel for expansion and only spending it on missions that are unusually lucrative or campaign-critical.
  • Once the campaign has multiple active regions, passing a mediocre mission is usually better than spending Intel to force it into viability.

Troop Ambush timing and region cycling

  • Troop Ambushes have a long cooldown after spawning. Both authors use a calendar/journal to estimate when the next one can appear and time a return to Intel collection accordingly.
  • Casey additionally notes a global cap on active Troop Ambushes, which can occasionally explain why a region is not producing one even when its local cooldown has passed.
  • After discovering a Troop Ambush in a high-Strength region, it can make sense to switch that haven away from Intel for a while, then return near the expected respawn window.

Black Market and economy

  • Early cash is more valuable than hoarding mediocre inventory. Both authors sell low-impact PCS, surplus weapon mods, data items and some corpses to fund facilities, staff and technology breakpoints.
  • Do not sell blindly: preserve corpses/wrecks needed for near-term autopsies/projects and the one-off resource gates in your chosen build order. The Sell/Keep lookup in this guide is intentionally conservative.
  • Casey’s older early-game economy is extremely aggressive about selling Cores/data/loot to fund urgent facilities, Vulture, staff and laser-tier purchases. Andy follows the same tempo logic but later becomes much more protective of Cores, Alloys and Elerium.
  • Start buying Elerium earlier than feels comfortable. Late-game weapon/armor progression is frequently bottlenecked by Elerium and Alloys rather than Supplies.
  • One or two liberated regions are generally enough for Supplies. More liberations can yield corpses and income, but they also remove unliberated regions that generate many of the missions that supply Alloys/Elerium.
  • Chosen weapons are usually poor research priorities unless acquired unusually early. Andy considers the Darkclaw the notable exception; normal Coil/Plasma progression typically gives better return on research time.

Facilities and campaign operations that are easy to forget

  • Keep the GTS relevant throughout the campaign; late GTS upgrades such as larger infiltrating squads are easy to overlook after the opening months.
  • Resistance Ring value rises after SGTs unlock better faction influence/rewards. Early low-value covert actions can consume scarce manpower; later, continuous covert operations become much more attractive.
  • Do not send valuable soldiers on weak covert actions while the barracks is shallow. Ambush chance is never literally zero unless the UI says so.
  • Templar HQ improves normal healing speed but not Tired recovery. Infirmary/Templar scanning also do not accelerate Will recovery from the Tired state.
  • Avenger travel timing — Andy observation: Andy’s 1.2.2 notes describe region-to-region flying as far less costly than players often assume because of how the geoscape clock behaves on arrival. Treat this as version-sensitive UI/strategy behavior rather than a precise universal timing rule; verify the current clock if a deadline is tight.
  • Keep a campaign journal. Andy explicitly uses it to identify patterns in failed missions, track Troop Ambush timing, and remember when strategic choices created later problems.
Troop Ambush global-cap edge case: Casey notes that only two Troop Ambushes can be active globally at once. If a high-Strength region seems overdue for one, check whether two are already active elsewhere before assuming your Intel setup is failing.
Casey 1.2 campaign observations retained as version-specific notes
  • Laboratory economics improved in 1.2. Once Black Market Scientist prices rise, Lab staffing can provide better research efficiency than buying every later Scientist. Early Scientists still matter because they satisfy technology/facility gates and early power/facility constraints can delay the Lab.
  • Troop Ambush timing shortcut: Casey’s 1.2 campaign estimates the next possible spawn by combining the 21-day local cooldown with the mission’s roughly 9–12-day availability window. Once an Ambush expires, another can often become eligible roughly 9–12 days later. Track the actual mission date in your campaign journal rather than treating this as an exact guarantee.
  • Casey also notes a global limit on simultaneous Troop Ambush opportunities; if another region is already holding the available slots, your preferred region can appear “late” even when its local cooldown has elapsed.
  • Stop selling Elerium Cores well before Elerium research. That research itself creates a sharp Elerium/Core gate, so the early-game “sell almost everything” rule has a clear expiration date.
  • Plasma Blaster valuation changed in 1.2: Casey values its two charges and broader practical endurance relative to the single-charge Shredder Gun; Andy still tends to favor Shredstorm once available. Preserve this as an author/playstyle split rather than a universal ranking.

4B. Covert Operations, Chosen clock & Golden Path

When Covert Ops become a real engine

  • Before the Resistance Ring, covert opportunities are limited. Once you have SGTs and better faction influence, the system becomes strategically important.
  • At Faction Influence 2, Moderate/Hard actions can include high-value rewards such as Recruit Scientist, Recruit Engineer and Gain Resistance Contact.
  • Once your barracks can sustain it, Casey wants the Ring running continuously: rewards, influence, ambush XP and contacts compound.
  • Do not send soldiers on low-value faction operations when manpower is scarce. The opportunity cost is an infiltrating squad, officer training or bond training.

Chosen Avenger Defense race

Three MSGTs are only the first deadline. After reaching three MSGTs you still need the Stronghold-reveal covert action (Casey cites roughly 13 days) and Stronghold infiltration (roughly 5 days). Counter Chosen Activity can buy time once a Chosen has found the Avenger, but Casey observed later delays becoming much less forgiving.

Golden Path operating rule

  1. After HQ, start opening the path to the Blacksite immediately.
  2. Ignore continent bonuses unless they happen to lie on the route.
  3. Carry a Skulljack Specialist regularly so Skullmining/hack opportunities generate Intel and accelerate strategic progress.
  4. After Blacksite, Casey prefers Psi Gate before Forge: you keep corpses on Psi Gate and it guarantees a Gatekeeper corpse.
  5. Most newly contacted havens can work Intel to help fund the next Golden Path contact chain.
  6. Do not treat the Avatar countdown as free extra time; hidden alien progress can resume immediately after you knock it back.

Laboratory decision

In Casey's older midgame plan the Lab makes sense once power and staffing can support it, ideally on/near a power-conduit solution. A serious Lab investment wants roughly six Scientists total—four staffing the Lab and two available for other facilities. His 1.2 note raises the Lab's value because Black Market Scientists become more expensive, but the warning remains: research can outrun the supplies/alloys/Elerium needed to buy what you unlock.

Source: Mid/Late Game Strategy, v1.2 Notes.

Covert-op scheduling rules that are easy to forget

  • Do not send soldiers on low-value faction actions until you have a manpower surplus. The hidden cost is a squad that cannot infiltrate, train as an officer, or complete bond training.
  • At Faction Influence 2, Moderate/Hard actions can produce Recruit Scientist, Recruit Engineer and Gain Resistance Contact; this is the point where the Ring becomes a genuine campaign engine.
  • Once the barracks is deep enough, Casey wants Covert Ops running almost continuously.
  • Counter Chosen Activity can delay an assault, but Casey observed the next attempt becoming much less forgiving. Treat it as borrowed time, not a permanent solution.

  1. Research Alien Biotech/implant line and build the Proving Ground as required by the story chain.
  2. Blacksite: revealed in a distant region, reinforcing why expansion matters. The vial advances the story and starts RNF pressure when picked up; the long doom timer means there is no need to rush natural pods.

Additional red-line warnings restored from Casey

Chosen Stronghold timing

Three MSGTs is not “safe.” You still need the reveal covert action and infiltration. Start power-leveling before the Avenger threat bar becomes urgent.

Low-value covert actions

Do not send scarce soldiers away simply because a faction mission exists. An unavailable soldier can cost a better infiltration, officer course or bond upgrade.

Strength 8 Hack / Snare

A routine-looking Hack in a very high-Strength region can become an RNF death spiral. Treat Strength 8+ Hack missions as a special hazard.

Fatigue lock

Officer/bond/trait training freezes Will recovery. Scheduling several trainings at once can quietly collapse your available roster.

Reaction-fire dependency

Casey's Ranger wipe story is a warning against assuming overwatch will solve a prolonged fight. Direct, controllable shots are more reliable when the battle stops following your script.

Panic play

When the fight starts collapsing, speeding up your decision-making compounds errors. Slow down, re-rank threats, and use control/guaranteed damage before gambling.

Andy campaign-ending failure patterns v6

  • Operating in Strength 8+ regions, especially hack missions that can become Snare traps.
  • Boosting routine missions until Intel expansion stalls. If boosts feel mandatory, you likely needed more regions earlier.
  • Taking 7 soldiers to routine Guerrilla Ops. The infiltration opportunity cost can silently destroy campaign tempo.
  • Chasing every Extract Supplies crate. Crate greed pulls soldiers into fog and away from evac as RNF accelerates.
  • Assuming a 90–99% action is guaranteed. Build turns with a backup action.
  • Using melee / point-blank damage into unexplored tiles. Assaults and Shinobis are especially good at tempting this campaign-ending mistake.
  • Ignoring Defense Matrix before an Avenger assault. LWOTC turrets are real combat assets when upgraded and staffed.
  • Entering a Ruler fight with other pods alive or spending tiny damage actions that each buy the Ruler another reaction.
  • Using Andy’s 1.2.2 SPARK build as if it were current 1.2.3 advice. It is explicitly version-invalidated.

16. Checklists

Before launching

  • Is the mission reward worth the roster/infiltration cost?
  • Is enemy activity acceptable?
  • Does this mission require 100% infiltration for the concealment you expect?
  • Scanner / scout?
  • Reliable killer?
  • At least 1–2 control options?
  • Objective Specialist if needed?
  • Armor shred / mechanical answer?
  • Chosen / Ruler burst plan?
  • Plating / vest / medikit?
  • RNF start condition understood?
  • Evac timing understood?

When a pod activates

  • Stop moving.
  • Count every active enemy.
  • Identify must-kill / must-control / can-ignore.
  • Check whether every proposed move reveals fog.
  • Use guaranteed control/damage before low-odds hero shots.
  • Protect the flank from the next pod.
  • Do not hoard the grenade/rocket if this is the hard fight.
  • If the situation worsens, slow your decision-making down.

Monthly strategic review

  • Do I have a low-Strength region to operate in?
  • Is first liberation / next Golden Path step moving?
  • Intel reserve sufficient for next contacts?
  • Are 3 soldiers being power-leveled toward MSGT?
  • Can I field 2–3 squads while training/covert ops continue?
  • Am I approaching an alloy / Elerium / core bottleneck?
  • Is a Chosen close to locating the Avenger?
  • Do I need Defense Matrix now?
