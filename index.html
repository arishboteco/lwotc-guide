SYNTHESIS

What this guide recommends

Where Casey and Andy differ, both positions are retained and a practical recommendation is added. The synthesis is deliberately labelled so it is never mistaken for either author’s published advice.

Important 1.2.3 caveat: Andy’s SPARK section was written for 1.2.2 and explicitly says it should be mostly ignored after the 1.2.3 SPARK rework. Use the rest of his 1.2.2 class advice as tested guidance, but treat SPARK as version-sensitive.
Honestman: Andy recommends saving normally but not reloading tactical mistakes, reserving saves for bugs/corruption. For a heavily modded first serious LWOTC campaign, this is a sensible safety practice.

1. The rules that keep campaigns alive

1 pod
Fight one pod at a time.

Most catastrophic turns begin when a melee move, flank, roof climb or careless scout activates another pod.

Scan first
Information is combat power.

In 1.2, Specialist Scanning Protocol changes campaign tempo. Use it after the first ambush to find remaining pods and protect flanks.

Control
Do not require every enemy to die this turn.

Burn, stun, hack, Stasis, Suppression and Sting Grenades convert impossible damage checks into manageable fights.

Deep barracks
Build more than one A-team.

Infiltration, wounds, covert actions, officer training and bond training all remove soldiers from availability.

Mission choice
You are not meant to run every mission.

Bad infiltration, high Strength and low-value rewards are valid reasons to pass.

Intel reserve
Expansion beats convenience.

Boost critical missions; do not routinely spend the Intel needed to open regions and reach Golden Path objectives.

Andy setup / mechanics notes worth knowing

  • Do not use Avenger-reskin mods in Andy’s setup; he reports they can break the game.
  • Resistance Orders are disabled by default in LWOTC stable 1.2.x.
  • Lost do not use vanilla Headshot chaining.
  • Training Center AP can buy eligible XCOM-row abilities beyond the soldier’s current rank.
  • Hero classes can bank a promotion by not selecting a perk immediately.
  • Laser sights in LWOTC increase crit chance without the vanilla distance falloff assumption Andy warns about.

1A. New-player essentials — the mechanics most likely to surprise you NEW IN v9

Source rule for this section: current stable LWOTC mechanics come first from UFOpaedia and the stable mod files. Andy, Casey and DerAva are used for strategy and explanation. Older LW2 / LWOTC 1.1 guides are useful for concepts, but their numbers are not copied unless they still match current mechanics.

Six systems to understand before a serious campaign

1. Missions must be discovered

Most opportunities already exist in the region; your haven has to detect them. Rebels on Intel generate detection, Avenger scanning is worth roughly 4 basic Intel workers, and a Scientist adviser adds a 20% haven-wide Intel bonus. More detection usually means you find missions earlier and get longer infiltration windows.

Haven & expansion details →

2. Strength ≠ Force ≠ Vigilance

Strength is regional ADVENT troop quantity and drives mission enemy counts. Force is global enemy quality/tech progression. Vigilance is ADVENT's alarm level caused mainly by XCOM activity; when Vigilance outruns Strength, ADVENT tends to move more Strength into the region.

Regional operating rules →

3. 100% infiltration removes major penalties

Below 100%, you can face more dangerous reinforcement queues, larger detection radii, slower evac and other mission pressure. Above 100% can improve several of those factors. Andy's beginner rule is sound: plan around 100% and skip missions that cannot justify under-infiltration.

Mission decision tool →

4. Call evac before you need it

The Skyranger is not immediate. Base evac delay is difficulty-dependent, and low infiltration / large squads can add turns. Throwing the flare breaks concealment; moving an existing evac zone adds arrival time. Once evac has arrived, it waits for you.

Evac & reinforcement rules →

5. Corpses are not normally recovered

Most guerrilla missions end by evacuation, so bodies stay behind. Look for Full Salvage missions such as Supply Raids, Troop Maneuvers/Columns, HQ assaults, landed UFOs and invasions when an autopsy or Proving Ground project needs a corpse.

Autopsy priorities →

6. You are expected to decline missions

A dark event or mediocre reward is often cheaper than a squad wipe. Andy's onboarding advice is to prefer missions that can infiltrate a useful 5+ soldier team, cancel an infiltration when a more important mission appears, and reserve Intel boosts mainly for campaign-critical or unusually valuable missions.

Base research days = RP cost ÷ [(10 + 5×unassigned Scientists + 10×Lab Scientists + 5×Basic Research completions) × 24]

How to acquire the bottleneck resources

Corpses & wrecks

Most evac missions do not recover bodies. Prioritize full-salvage missions such as Troop Maneuvers / troop-column ambushes, Supply Line Raids, regional HQ assaults and other full-salvage fights when you need a specific autopsy corpse. Explosive killing blows can destroy recoverable materiel, so preserve key targets when practical.

Alloys / Elerium / Cores

Full-salvage missions are the cleanest source. Smash N Grab resource caches can also yield Supplies, Alloys, Elerium or Cores even though they do not return corpses. Use the Black Market to bridge a breakpoint, not to erase every shortage.

Intel

Keep enough for Resistance Communications/Radio and Golden Path research before spending it on routine mission boosts. Save datapads/caches when a large contact or Shadow Chamber bill is approaching.

Research capacity

A Scientist produces value every day you acquire them early. Once you can support a mature Lab, lab staffing doubles each staffed Scientist’s research contribution from 5 to 10 RP/hour. Do not over-tech so far ahead that you cannot afford the equipment you just unlocked.

Decision rule when you miss a monthly threshold: protect the next weapon/armor breakpoint, then the Golden Path requirement, then convenience research. A delayed Purifier/Viper/Faceless autopsy is usually recoverable; entering a new force-level band while a full weapon tier behind is much harder to recover from.

3. Priority purchases — items, personnel & upgrades

This section owns shopping decisions only. Facilities and budget arithmetic live in Facility & Cost Plan; research lives in Research Roadmap.
Casey gives a priority list rather than a strict chronological shopping list. The order below is my synthesis of that list, his 1.2 notes, and the economic bottlenecks he identifies.

March–early April: survival and compounding value

  1. Early Engineer. Casey generally favors an Engineer over a Scientist when counts are equal; he wants roughly three Engineers quickly. In 1.2, Alloy Plating also requires two Engineers.
  2. Ablative protection. Alloy Plating project when available; build enough Nanoscale/Plating that core soldiers are not taking naked lethal rolls.
  3. Wet Work. XP acceleration pays back across dozens of missions.
  4. Basic consumable package. Rough early squad target from Casey: about 3 flashbangs, 4 grenades and 1–2 medikits on a 6-person squad.
  5. AP Rounds ×3. Early mechanical answer, especially strong on Ranger.
  6. Vulture. Casey calls this very important to obtain soon because extra loot funds later purchases.

4. Regions, havens and expansion

Starting region

  • Start with essentially everyone on Intel.
  • Assign a Haven Advisor immediately; Casey likes Ranger or Assault early because they handle Faceless well.
  • His early routine: scan the starting region until you have found roughly two useful missions, then use Black Market scanning time.
  • Jailbreaks usually provide enough new rebels; Recruiting is situational rather than a permanent job.
  • When rebels change jobs they lose roughly a day of productivity, so do not constantly shuffle Intel ↔ Supply.

When to open the first new region

Practical trigger: contact an adjacent region as soon as Resistance Communications and your Intel budget allow — ideally while the starting region is still a productive low-Strength mission farm, not after it is already at Strength 5.

Casey's explicit rule is to expand quickly. Your starting region accumulates Vigilance because you are doing missions there. ADVENT responds by increasing Strength. You want the next low-Strength region operational before standard missions in the first become unattractive.

Strength 5+ treatment

  • At about Strength 5, Supply gathering becomes a reasonable alternative because normal missions become harder to execute.
  • Intel is still useful in hot regions for Troop Ambushes and stealthable extraction/hack opportunities.
  • Casey often hunts a Troop Ambush, then switches the haven to Supplies for a couple of weeks, then returns to Intel when another ambush can plausibly spawn.
  • He tracks Troop Ambush timing: roughly a 21-day cooldown from spawn; his 1.2 notes simplify the practical post-expiry window to roughly 9–12 days + remaining time on the current ambush.
  • Only two Troop Ambushes can be active globally at once in the older framework he describes.

Liberation region

  • Once you choose a liberation region, put a large share — often all — rebels on Intel when hunting Lib 2/3.
  • Do not run every unrelated mission that appears there. Extra activity raises Vigilance/Strength and makes the liberation missions harder.
  • Lib 2 is hard to detect; Casey's classic approach is to let bad-timer versions expire and keep searching for a version with enough time to send a full team with an Intel boost.

After first liberation

  • First liberation target: approximately July.
  • Once HQ is beaten and the Blacksite is revealed, start expanding toward it immediately.
  • Ignore continent bonuses unless they are conveniently on the Golden Path route.
  • Expect the Blacksite route to be expensive. Casey gives an example path costing about 500 Intel plus a 350-supply relay.
  • After Blacksite, repeat the same logic for Forge and Psi Gate regions.
  • Resistance Communications can often be delayed because covert actions and points of interest can provide contacts.

Early scanning & Troop Ambush timing — exact notes

  • Start with essentially all rebels on Intel, assign a Haven Advisor, and scan the starting region until roughly two missions are found; then spend scan time at the Black Market.
  • Changing a rebel's job causes roughly 24 hours of lost productivity; avoid constant Intel ↔ Supply toggling.
  • Older Casey timing: Troop Ambushes have a 21-day cooldown from spawn and only two can be active globally.
  • 1.2 practical estimate: once an Ambush expires, another can become eligible in roughly 9–12 days. While one is active, estimate next eligibility as 9–12 days + current mission time remaining.
  • At high Strength Casey often keeps Intel long enough to find the Ambush, then flips to Supplies for a couple of weeks before searching again.

Free early class-selection trick

The first eight normal XCOM promotions contain one of each base class. Trial by Fire — how it actually awards XP

  • Trial by Fire applies before mission XP. It first grants enough XP for an eligible low-rank soldier to reach the next rank; mission XP is then added afterward.
  • That means even a soldier only a few XP short of promotion can still benefit from accompanying a Trial by Fire officer.
  • Kill XP is awarded during the mission, so kills reduce the later Trial by Fire grant. If you are deliberately power-leveling trainees, let veterans do more of the killing when tactically safe.
  • Rush several soldiers to SGT: SGT unlocks important faction influence/contact options and Trial by Fire officer development.

4B. Covert Operations, Chosen clock & Golden Path

When Covert Ops become a real engine

  • Before the Resistance Ring, covert opportunities are limited. Once you have SGTs and better faction influence, the system becomes strategically important.
  • At Faction Influence 2, Moderate/Hard actions can include high-value rewards such as Recruit Scientist, Recruit Engineer and Gain Resistance Contact.
  • Once your barracks can sustain it, Casey wants the Ring running continuously: rewards, influence, ambush XP and contacts compound.
  • Do not send soldiers on low-value faction operations when manpower is scarce. The opportunity cost is an infiltrating squad, officer training or bond training.

Chosen Avenger Defense race

Three MSGTs are only the first deadline. After reaching three MSGTs you still need the Stronghold-reveal covert action (Casey cites roughly 13 days) and Stronghold infiltration (roughly 5 days). Counter Chosen Activity can buy time once a Chosen has found the Avenger, but Casey observed later delays becoming much less forgiving.

Golden Path operating rule

  1. After HQ, start opening the path to the Blacksite immediately.
  2. Ignore continent bonuses unless they happen to lie on the route.
  3. Carry a Skulljack Specialist regularly so Skullmining/hack opportunities generate Intel and accelerate strategic progress.
  4. After Blacksite, Casey prefers Psi Gate before Forge: you keep corpses on Psi Gate and it guarantees a Gatekeeper corpse.
  5. Most newly contacted havens can work Intel to help fund the next Golden Path contact chain.
  6. Do not treat the Avatar countdown as free extra time; hidden alien progress can resume immediately after you knock it back.

Laboratory decision

In Casey's older midgame plan the Lab makes sense once power and staffing can support it, ideally on/near a power-conduit solution. A serious Lab investment wants roughly six Scientists total—four staffing the Lab and two available for other facilities. His 1.2 note raises the Lab's value because Black Market Scientists become more expensive, but the warning remains: research can outrun the supplies/alloys/Elerium needed to buy what you unlock.

Source: Mid/Late Game Strategy, v1.2 Notes.

Covert-op scheduling rules that are easy to forget

  • Do not send soldiers on low-value faction actions until you have a manpower surplus. The hidden cost is a squad that cannot infiltrate, train as an officer, or complete bond training.
  • At Faction Influence 2, Moderate/Hard actions can produce Recruit Scientist, Recruit Engineer and Gain Resistance Contact; this is the point where the Ring becomes a genuine campaign engine.
  • Once the barracks is deep enough, Casey wants Covert Ops running almost continuously.
  • Counter Chosen Activity can delay an assault, but Casey observed the next attempt becoming much less forgiving. Treat it as borrowed time, not a permanent solution.

5. Mission selection — stage-by-stage decision matrix

This matrix is a practical synthesis, not a literal Casey scoring formula. Liberation-region gateOnce a region is your Liberation target, unrelated missions have an extra hidden cost: they raise Vigilance and can make Lib 2/3 harder. Skip mediocre side missions there.

Extract Supplies: concealed WOTC-style crate race; Andy considers it substantially riskier. Andy’s rough full-haul example is about 75 supplies + 14 alloys + 14 Elerium, but 1.2.3 rebalanced these reward schedules; treat the number as historical/illustrative, not a guaranteed current payout.

Supply Raid / Troop Ambush

Exceptional reward, exceptional wipe potential. Andy generally wants <20 expected enemies, a strong team and good equipment. This is one of the few mission families he will boost, send more than six to, or even pull the A-team from another infiltration for.

Do not hoard rockets/incendiaries when a giant rainbow pod or multiple pods collide. A healthy squad with no consumables is preferable to a wounded squad that “saved” ordinance.

Covert Op Ambush

Always send the covert pair with a medikit. Technicals and Shinobis are strong ambush-risk candidates because Squaddies still bring fire control/scouting. The four-person rescue team values mobility and immediate impact: SPARK, Skirmisher and a Specialist with Medical Protocol can be excellent.

The stranded pair should avoid waking pods until rescuers close the distance; when forced to fight, use control to buy time rather than expecting a B/C-equipment pair to alpha-strike a full pod.

Sabotage Transmitter vs Destroy Relay

Destroy Relay: use concealment to approach and set the ambush; RNF pressure starts after reveal. Sabotage Transmitter: very short objective timer, so take the first decent ambush and use relays to buy time. Do not extend the objective timer so long that the RNF clock kills you.

Blacksite / Facility

Throw evac earlier than intuition suggests; facility evac can be very slow. For Blacksite, Andy prefers clearing the map, staging almost everyone near evac, then sending the fast soldier for the vial because taking the vial starts reinforcement pressure. The 45-turn doom timer is generous: do not rush the map.

UFO / Chosen Avenger Defense

Defense Matrix is extremely valuable: upgraded, staffed turrets can provide eight meaningful shots per turn. On Chosen Defense, do not get sucked into racing the objective; Andy reports enough Avenger power for careful play. The 1.2 version is far more beatable than old LWOTC versions.

8. Tactical operating procedure

Leapfrog movement

Move a rear soldier into the lead, then another rear soldier into the lead. You can often move the squad a full blue move while exposing only a few tiles of new fog. This is especially important on 2-man stealth missions and timed maps.

Flashbang doctrine

  • Casey takes roughly three flashbangs per mission for the first two months.
  • Flash the Sectoid, not the mind-controlled soldier or zombie, to break mind control / dispel the zombie.
  • Flashbangs disable Stun Lancer melee and reduce many enemies to low-threat shooters.
  • Non-Grenadiers are good flash carriers because flashbang throw range is naturally long; let Grenadiers use their range advantage on actual grenades.

RNF timer rule

If RNF is already ticking: take a good first fight quickly. If RNF begins on reveal: move close to the objective before breaking concealment.

Five mission phases

  1. Move in concealment: preserve concealment, watch hidden civilians, give building corners a wide berth, use SMG mobility, and use Specialist hacks to disable a blocking Drone without necessarily breaking concealment.
  2. Ambush pod 1: do not wait for perfection if RNF is active. Flamethrower can be a dominant opener; grenade ambush is fine, but follow with guaranteed damage. Keep a scout concealed when possible.
  3. Move without concealment: slow down. Scouts need deliberate LOS-safe moves. Use sound cues, LOS-breaking terrain and Scanner. Overwatch creeping every turn is too slow.
  4. Fight pod 2 at range: this is often the hardest fight. Rockets/Grenadier cover destruction, Combat Protocol, Electroshock and suppression matter. Do not hoard ordinance if you need it now.
  5. Final pod / unchained movement: once every remaining enemy location is known, melee and aggressive movement become much safer. This is the Assault/Shinobi moment.

1.2 scouting update

  • Scanning Protocol is now Casey's preferred primary information tool on most concealed missions once the Gremlin is upgraded.
  • Do not waste scans before the ambush unless necessary; a forward blue move before scanning increases coverage.
  • Shinobi still scouts long missions, non-concealed starts, stealth ops and situations where a Sharpshooter needs a spotter.
  • Bad scouting is worse than no scouting. Casey repeatedly warns that rushing corners/roofs can end campaigns.

Concealment → ambush → second pod → final pod: micro rules

While concealed

  • Assume civilians may be hidden behind high-cover objects at the insertion area.
  • Give building corners and other LOS blockers a wide berth.
  • SMG users can fan out a little while concealed to help find the first pod.
  • A Specialist can disable/hack a Drone without necessarily breaking squad concealment.
  • If sneaking around pods, the far map edge is often the safest route.

First ambush

  • If RNF is already ticking, take a good enough ambush instead of wasting turns.
  • If RNF has not started, get close to the objective first.
  • Preserve the Shinobi/Reaper scout when possible.
  • Do not risk soldiers for loot; guaranteed grenade/Combat Protocol damage is worth using to close the fight.

Moving revealed

  • Yellow-alert enemies can sometimes shoot on activation; avoid exposing flanks to a pod drawn by noise.
  • Rooftops can be excellent Shinobi scouting positions when the approach is safe.
  • If the RNF warning turns red, hide the concealed Shinobi before the drop and then move/retreat quickly.
  • When forced to double-move blind, move a lower-ranked soldier first into strong cover.
  • Move two-action fighters such as Sharpshooter and rocket Technical only after safer scouts/movers have shown the path.

Second pod at range

  • Do not hoard rockets/grenades for a hypothetical later battle; this is often the hardest engagement.
  • Electroshock is usually preferable to charging an Assault into unknown fog.
  • A Grenadier + Specialist can create guaranteed long-range damage/cover destruction even when ordinary shots are poor.

1.2 edge-case warnings

  • Enemy pods can patrol roughly 8 tiles in a straight normal move; do not assume a pod is safely out of reach because it looked distant last turn.
  • Purifier flamethrower is not disabled by Flashbang.
  • Berserker and Archon melee are not disabled by burning when they can still reach a target.
  • Stunning a Spectre does not release an already-Shadowbound soldier.
  • Suppression is not a perfect answer to ADVENT Rocketeers; they can sometimes run it and still launch a rocket.
  • Late-game ADVENT can gain Covering Fire; movement/action sequencing around overwatch becomes more dangerous.

Andy’s probability / consistency doctrine v6

ANDY A 90% shot is not “guaranteed”; across hundreds of shots, misses are inevitable. Tactical quality means arranging turns so a single miss does not decide the squad’s fate. Distinguish bad outcome from a good decision from bad outcome caused by a bad decision.

Equipment / movement micro

  • Do not sleep on plating. Ablative HP prevents both deaths and long wound timers.
  • An empty utility slot can be treated as an intentional +1 Mobility item.
  • For Andy’s control builds, SMGs commonly go to Shinobi, Technical, Grenadier and most Specialists; mobility matters more than rifle damage they rarely use.
  • Early AP Rounds are extremely valuable on primary shooters.
  • Once Skullmining exists, a Specialist’s Skulljack is also a hack-stat tool, not only a story item.
  • Hacking a robot does not break concealment.
  • Stunning an enemy removes its detection radius, which can rescue a concealed Shinobi caught too close.

Two-person stealth hack — Andy’s execution

  1. Use Shinobi + Specialist, usually with SMGs and minimal weight.
  2. Scout a safe line-of-sight tile to the objective.
  3. Throw evac before committing; this may reveal the Specialist, so the Shinobi must keep the route safe.
  4. Hack and, if needed, use Command/Teamwork to pull the Specialist back out.
  5. A smoke grenade can buy a desperate turn, but if the pair is revealed before evac setup, survival odds collapse.
Do not generalize stealth hacks to revealed Extract VIP missions. Wandering patrols and overwatch make a lone-scout extraction much less reliable.

Hack rewards / failure reminders

Andy tracks high-impact hack effects such as all-robot control, temporary enemy control, disorient, action-point refund, concealment, targeting/crit buffs and supply/Intel caches. Failure effects can include Map Alert, Reinforcements or enemy stat buffs. Map Alert is not always disastrous if you are already in a defensive position and prefer enemies to come to you.

Network Tower — exact setup

  1. Run it soon after revealing it; ADVENT Strength can continue climbing.
  2. Casey likes extra infiltration when it meaningfully lowers enemy activity/detection radius, but in LWOTC he is less willing to burn Intel just to push an already-manageable Tower from Light-Moderate to Light.
  3. A Ghostwalker + Covert Shinobi remains uniquely good because this mission is about physically slipping past pods, not merely knowing where they are.
  4. Spider Suit is a specific escape trick: Grapple can pull the Shinobi out of a detection trap without alerting the pod.
  5. When the Shinobi can reach the console in one turn, reposition the other four soldiers—ideally toward the Command Center.
  6. Triggering the console creates a two-turn enemy stun window. Use it to erase the Command Pod and nearby pods before they recover.
  7. The Assault is especially good at immediately deleting the Command Pod once the stun lands.

ADVENT HQ — endurance doctrine

  • Expect roughly 40+ enemies; Casey recommends a full LWOTC team around eight soldiers for a first/serious attempt.
  • Bring many Medkits and expect to use them. EXO suits/extra ordnance are strong, but preserve at least one serious control charge for the General.
  • Find a strong defensive roof/position and let multiple pods come to you rather than advancing after every kill.
  • Avoid pulling double-Drone pods or the Command Pod into the main firefight if you can help it.
  • Only hunt the Command Pod after the rest of the map is nearly cleared.

General control cheat sheet

Assault Stun · Flamethrower · Incendiary · Stasis · Sting. Casey's cleanest loop is Stun → Stasis → Stun, exploiting Stun's one-turn cooldown. An aggressive alternative is Shred → Rapid Fire Assault → Officer Command.

Invasion — timing and preparation

  • An Invasion cannot occur until at least 21 days after Liberation.
  • It also requires a neighboring region around Strength 8+; a growing adjacent strength stack is your warning that an invasion force may be forming.
  • Once the 21-day window opens, put a strong, equipped soldier in the liberated haven as advisor.
  • Primary beacon plan: Stealth Shinobi gets vision while Sharpshooter destroys the beacon. Roofed beacons can sometimes be solved by destroying the support/roof or by long-range rifle fire.
  • Fallbacks include rockets/Bombard, a stealth melee approach, or brute-force mobile killers.
  • LWOTC version is milder than old LW2; Casey sometimes recommends delaying the final beacon kill long enough to farm a few safe reinforcement drops/corpses, then ending it before the battle becomes unstable.

Andy’s Network Tower execution v6

  • Andy treats Tower as the one mission that is effectively stealth-mandatory: only five soldiers and aggressive post-reveal RNF.
  • Move the Shinobi decisively; excessive dawdling creates more patrol risk. Sergeant/Covert is valuable, Ghostwalker Shinobi is an ideal specialist if you happen to have one.
  • Approach the final building from the right (right while facing it from spawn): Andy notes the left side can contain a turret while the right side does not in his observed layouts.
  • Stage combat troops so each is within a blue move of a pod before hacking. The stun window is wasted if everyone must spend it running.
  • Trigger the computer on the first action of the turn whenever possible.
  • Andy describes the stun as effectively giving three XCOM turns of advantage: activation turn, following stunned turn, then the enemy’s wake-up turn without normal action before XCOM acts again.
  • Codices and Sustaining Priests can behave as exceptions; plan them as priority cleanup.
  • Do not put SMGs on the entire team just to sneak; after the hack you still must kill the map.
CASEY

Specialist + Grenadier + Shinobi + Ranger + Assault is the published 1.2 recommendation.

ANDY

Shinobi core; Reaper (preferably Banish), Skirmisher, Assault/Ranger, Grenadier, with SPARK/offensive Psi/early Technical as alternatives. Andy questions a Specialist because the objective placement prevents the “safe remote hack” people imagine and he values post-hack damage.

Andy’s HQ Assault operating doctrine

  • Aim for roughly 8 strong soldiers; 9 is possible, 10 often imposes too much infiltration opportunity cost.
  • Claim a defensible building early and make pods come to you.
  • Keep the Shinobi concealed until you are approaching the General / final critical engagement.
  • Prefer repeatable cooldown abilities and extended magazines; long missions exhaust limited consumables and auto-loader charges.
  • Use consumables without guilt on the critical turn when two or three pods collide.
  • HQ destroys up to 5 regional Strength; doing it at Strength 5 maximizes that effect, but never delay an important first liberation just to optimize the number.
  • Completing HQ gives other active infiltrations in the region an immediate +50% infiltration, but they must be launched or aborted then—occasionally exploitable when planning parallel missions.

Invasion

Andy also uses the 21-day post-liberation floor and adjacent Strength 8+ spike as the warning. Unlike some older LW2 advice, he does not consider a soldier advisor mandatory solely for the invasion and prefers an Engineer for liberated-region supplies until the fight is actually coming. Once the mission starts, advance aggressively enough to destroy the beacon shortly after constant RNF begins; killing the Chosen early simplifies the entire defense.

13. Chosen and Alien Rulers

Chosen — universal doctrine

  • Early/midgame Chosen are primarily a burst-damage problem; normal hard-control tools do not simply shut them down.
  • Burn can disable selected abilities; Suppression remains useful; Venom can slow.
  • Casey's burst progression includes Both Barrels, Amplify, Skirmisher/Gunner Chain Shot, Cyclic Fire, Assault/Ranger Rapid Fire and Reaper Banish.
  • Mag tier is a major Chosen damage breakpoint, especially because it upgrades the sawed-off burst option.

Warlock

  • Greatest Champion means kill the champion first.
  • Sometimes burning minions instead of killing them prevents immediate resummons.
  • Burn disables CCS; Gunner suppression is useful for approaching.
  • Attack with farthest/least-mobile soldiers first if you are worried he will retreat.
  • 1.2 trick: time the final summon death so Stasis can end just before your turn.

Hunter

  • Grapple creates constant flanks and retreating movement.
  • Farsight overwatch does not give the normal obvious movement warning; bring remote overwatch removal (Combat Protocol, Soul Fire, grenades, rockets).
  • Burn disables the pistol ability and helps protect resistance personnel.
  • Fight mobility with mobility and long-range force projection.

Assassin

  • Scanning Protocol is Casey's strongest anti-hide tool.
  • On Haven Defense it may be better to handle immediate ADVENT first rather than spend the whole turn searching.
  • 1.2 changes made her damage-reduction mechanics easier to manage than older versions.
  • Do not let the hunt for her fragment the squad.

Alien Ruler sequence

  1. Bring Psi Stasis, Grenadier Sting/Frost/Incendiary, Assault Stun Gunner and at least two serious shooters with shred.
  2. Scout until the Ruler pod is isolated.
  3. Position the entire squad before activation.
  4. If unconcealed, an overwatch trap can give free damage before Ruler Reactions.
  5. Open with control grenade; use shred while the Ruler is contained.
  6. Put the Ruler in Stasis before it can act.
  7. Move Assault into guaranteed-stun range for the next turn.
  8. Next turn: stun → unload damage → chain another control if necessary.
  9. Officer actions from a still-hidden Shinobi can be used without triggering Ruler Reactions in Casey's older sequence.

Micro-techniques Casey uses against Chosen

  • Demolition and Bombard can remove cover without damaging the Chosen, avoiding the normal damage-triggered reposition while still exposing them.
  • Venom Rounds and Iron Curtain slow movement when hard immobilization is unavailable.
  • Burning often removes important secondary actions; Suppression lowers Aim and can discourage Hunter/Warlock movement. If Suppression is doing its job, do not automatically flank and trigger a reposition that breaks it.

Warlock manipulation

  • Greatest Champion: remove the champion before expecting clean damage on the Warlock.
  • When he is alone, killing every minion immediately can simply invite another summon; sometimes burning/control is better than deletion.
  • You can encourage Mind Scorch by presenting clustered targets that tolerate the effect—e.g., fire-immune/tanky bait—rather than giving him a premium isolated victim.
  • Burning disables his CCS, making approach much safer.

Hunter manipulation

  • Farsight Overwatch lacks the normal obvious danger indicator. Break it remotely with Combat Protocol, Soul Fire, explosives/rockets or other non-movement answers.
  • Bring mobility and force projection; forcing the squad to chase slowly while he grapples backward is how the fight becomes expensive.

Assassin search criteria

Do not spend the turn searching just because she is hidden. Casey's test is roughly: search only when (A) no more urgent enemies are active, (B) you can deal meaningful damage after revealing her, and (C) you can apply fire/control that prevents her bonus-action snowball. Scanning Protocol is the strongest current detection tool.

Alien Ruler reaction rules Casey explicitly notes

  • A Ruler gets a reaction when a visible soldier performs an action in its sight, including moving into visible range.
  • Reloading does not trigger a Ruler reaction in Casey's note; actions by concealed soldiers also do not trigger it.
  • An Advanced Arc Thrower is particularly valuable because the extra stun action meaningfully suppresses more Ruler reactions; the basic Arc Thrower's one-action stun is much weaker against Ruler action economy.
  • Maim is worth looking for on XCOM-row perks when dealing with the Berserker Queen.

Assassin — do not reveal her just because you can

Casey's v1.1 note is deliberately counter-intuitive: revealing the Assassin can give her a more dangerous two-action turn than allowing her to break stealth naturally. Search only when (A) other urgent enemies are handled, (B) you can deal meaningful damage after revealing, and (C) you can apply fire/other mitigation immediately.

Andy Chosen refinements v6

Assassin — treat her like a predictable mechanical system

Do not cluster for Harbor Wave. Avoid adjacent Assault/Shinobi attacks unless they kill: Prime Reaction will punish the adjacent unit and increase later damage. Andy kites her by attacking from farther soldiers so she moves toward the nearest target who still has an action to retreat. Once her per-turn damage-reduction threshold kicks in, either finish decisively or stop feeding inefficient attacks.

Warlock — summons are the real threat

Spread out against Mind Scorch fire. Suppression or burning can shut down Close Combat Specialist. Assume attacking late in your turn may produce several summoned enemies. If Spectral Lancers are active, killing the final lancer by overwatch during the alien turn can force the Warlock out of Stasis too late to act, handing you a full turn.

Chosen Stronghold — damage race, not scout mission

Five soldiers. Andy does not bring his normal Shinobi officer; raw firepower and multi-shot abilities matter more. Clear patrolling ADVENT before walking far enough to spawn the Chosen. Reset cooldowns before the Ascension Gate if useful. Once the Chosen falls, burn the sarcophagus immediately: guaranteed hit chance makes Banish/multi-shot attacks exceptional. When the Chosen returns, if you can kill them this turn, ignore other enemies—the mission ends on the Chosen’s death.

Alien Ruler — Andy’s stun/burst script

  1. Scout the Ruler after clearing as much of the map as possible.
  2. Position everyone; soldiers outside LOS can prepare without provoking reactions.
  3. Delete one escort from long range if possible.
  4. Use upgraded Arc Thrower / Disabling Shot to bank multiple safe actions.
  5. Only spend actions on the Ruler when they deliver meaningful burst—Rapid Fire, Cyclic Fire, Banish, etc.
  6. Use the next stun before the previous safe-action window disappears; count remaining stun actions explicitly.
  7. Do not give the Ruler a reaction in exchange for trivial chip damage.

Additional red-line warnings restored from Casey

Chosen Stronghold timing

Three MSGTs is not “safe.” You still need the reveal covert action and infiltration. Start power-leveling before the Avenger threat bar becomes urgent.

Low-value covert actions

Do not send scarce soldiers away simply because a faction mission exists. An unavailable soldier can cost a better infiltration, officer course or bond upgrade.

Strength 8 Hack / Snare

A routine-looking Hack in a very high-Strength region can become an RNF death spiral. Treat Strength 8+ Hack missions as a special hazard.

Fatigue lock

Officer/bond/trait training freezes Will recovery. Scheduling several trainings at once can quietly collapse your available roster.

Reaction-fire dependency

Casey's Ranger wipe story is a warning against assuming overwatch will solve a prolonged fight. Direct, controllable shots are more reliable when the battle stops following your script.

Panic play

When the fight starts collapsing, speeding up your decision-making compounds errors. Slow down, re-rank threats, and use control/guaranteed damage before gambling.

Andy campaign-ending failure patterns v6

  • Operating in Strength 8+ regions, especially hack missions that can become Snare traps.
  • Boosting routine missions until Intel expansion stalls. If boosts feel mandatory, you likely needed more regions earlier.
  • Taking 7 soldiers to routine Guerrilla Ops. The infiltration opportunity cost can silently destroy campaign tempo.
  • Chasing every Extract Supplies crate. Crate greed pulls soldiers into fog and away from evac as RNF accelerates.
  • Assuming a 90–99% action is guaranteed. Build turns with a backup action.
  • Using melee / point-blank damage into unexplored tiles. Assaults and Shinobis are especially good at tempting this campaign-ending mistake.
  • Ignoring Defense Matrix before an Avenger assault. LWOTC turrets are real combat assets when upgraded and staffed.
  • Entering a Ruler fight with other pods alive or spending tiny damage actions that each buy the Ruler another reaction.
  • Using Andy’s 1.2.2 SPARK build as if it were current 1.2.3 advice. It is explicitly version-invalidated.

16. Checklists

Before launching

  • Is the mission reward worth the roster/infiltration cost?
  • Is enemy activity acceptable?
  • Does this mission require 100% infiltration for the concealment you expect?
  • Scanner / scout?
  • Reliable killer?
  • At least 1–2 control options?
  • Objective Specialist if needed?
  • Armor shred / mechanical answer?
  • Chosen / Ruler burst plan?
  • Plating / vest / medikit?
  • RNF start condition understood?
  • Evac timing understood?

When a pod activates

  • Stop moving.
  • Count every active enemy.
  • Identify must-kill / must-control / can-ignore.
  • Check whether every proposed move reveals fog.
  • Use guaranteed control/damage before low-odds hero shots.
  • Protect the flank from the next pod.
  • Do not hoard the grenade/rocket if this is the hard fight.
  • If the situation worsens, slow your decision-making down.

Monthly strategic review

  • Do I have a low-Strength region to operate in?
  • Is first liberation / next Golden Path step moving?
  • Intel reserve sufficient for next contacts?
  • Are 3 soldiers being power-leveled toward MSGT?
  • Can I field 2–3 squads while training/covert ops continue?
  • Am I approaching an alloy / Elerium / core bottleneck?
  • Is a Chosen close to locating the Avenger?
  • Do I need Defense Matrix now?
