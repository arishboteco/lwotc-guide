4A. Stats, class selection, fatigue & promotion tempo

Not Created Equally — source evolution: Casey's older Early Game Strategy preferred turning NCE off for a more standardized roster and pointed to Choose My Class as a legacy alternative for controlling rookie class assignment. His 2026 v1.2 campaign deliberately left NCE on and then developed the stat-triage rules below. Treat this as a changed preference, not a contradiction to hide: the modern advice is how to exploit random stats if you play with NCE enabled, while the older NCE-off / Choose-My-Class route remains a labelled legacy option.

Free early class-selection trick

The first eight normal XCOM promotions contain one of each base class. After the first seven promotions you know the eighth class in advance, so you can deliberately bring a rookie whose stats suit that remaining class. Sometimes after six promotions the two remaining classes want similar stat profiles; Casey may wait to promote rookies seven/eight until the next missions return.

Trial by Fire — how it actually awards XP

  • Trial by Fire applies before mission XP. It first grants enough XP for an eligible low-rank soldier to reach the next rank; mission XP is then added afterward.
  • That means even a soldier only a few XP short of promotion can still benefit from accompanying a Trial by Fire officer.
  • Kill XP is awarded during the mission, so kills reduce the later Trial by Fire grant. If you are deliberately power-leveling trainees, let veterans do more of the killing when tactically safe.
  • Rush several soldiers to SGT: SGT unlocks important faction influence/contact options and Trial by Fire officer development.

Do not stat-max your campaign into paralysis. Casey explicitly warns that a high-Aim rookie can still die, get wounded repeatedly, roll weak XCOM perks, or simply fail to level. Combat Intelligence, Will and Mobility matter; Psi is the exception where Psi Offense dominates the class decision.
Casey 1.2 random-stat / class-assignment edge cases
  • Hold roughly six rookies for deliberate GTS class selection when Not Created Equally is enabled. Holding many more can starve early missions; holding none wastes the chance to match rare stat profiles to the classes that use them best.
  • Mark the best 2–3 Psi Offense rookies for Psi training first. Psi Offense matters much more than conventional Aim for this class; low Mobility is far less damaging once Phase Walk is available.
  • Save several high-Aim rookies for Ranger/Gunner/Sharpshooter and consider unusually high Hack for Specialist, but do not optimize only Aim: Mobility, Will and Combat Intelligence can matter just as much to long-term value.
  • First-eight-promotion trick: the first eight normal rookie promotions are distributed one per conventional XCOM class. After seven classes have appeared, the eighth promotion is predictable. You can exploit this by bringing a rookie whose stats suit the remaining class. Sometimes after six promotions, if the two remaining classes want similar stat profiles, it is worth delaying both promotions until suitable rookies return from missions.
  • Do not build a campaign around one “perfect” rookie. Death, wounds, negative traits and weak randomized XCOM-row rolls can erase the apparent advantage. The goal is to improve roster odds, not stat-max every soldier.
  • Advanced Focus chips are a cheap early option for poor-Will soldiers when the barracks is shallow and Tired recovery is restricting availability.

Specialist — Hack 'n Heal 1.2 revised

Andy trades Casey’s late healing insurance for more Gremlin offense. If the campaign is taking heavy wounds, Casey’s Savior/Restoration route remains safer.

EQUIPMENT

Usually SMG + upgraded Gremlin + plating. Add Medikit for Medical Protocol and Skulljack for Golden Path/hacking. Hack PCS for support/hacker. If you deliberately convert to the Overwatch archetype, use Rifle + Scope + Expanded Magazine and prioritize Aim.

Complete source notes — Specialist
  • Class identity: both authors treat Specialist as the broadest utility package rather than a damage class: scouting, hacking, healing, objective interaction, overwatch clearing and mechanical control. Its weakness is direct damage, which is why officer duty can be attractive.
  • Scanning Protocol changes squad structure. Casey’s 1.2 revision treats it as transformative: use it after the first ambush to locate remaining pods, allowing the Shinobi to join combat. It scales from roughly 2 charges on the early Gremlin to more charges on upgraded Gremlins, so do not waste early scans before contact unless the map demands it.
  • Revival alternative: every soldier has the two-action Get Up option for an unconscious ally, which further reduces the need to spend a Specialist perk solely on Revival Protocol. Revival still has value when action economy or Chosen kidnapping pressure makes the generic action too slow.
  • Andy likewise finds Scanning Protocol useful throughout the campaign and notes that it can let a Specialist substitute for a Shinobi on some small missions. Revival remains defensible for early Chosen kidnapping prevention, but becomes less necessary as play improves.
  • Interference Protocol is free Overwatch cancellation and should be used liberally on normal missions. In stable 1.2.3 it cannot be spammed multiple times in the same turn, but that rarely changes the decision.
  • Trojan extends shutdown utility and can kill/soften Drones/MECs; Drone hacks can be performed without breaking concealment. Andy values it above Field Surgeon despite Field Surgeon being very good for roster availability.
  • Medical Protocol remains emergency combat healing at range. Casey is more healing-friendly after several disaster missions; Andy has moved toward “healing is recovery from a mistake” and prefers a more offensive late tree, but still takes Medical Protocol because a single preventable death is worth the slot.
  • Airdrop is best when a Grenadier/Skirmisher is present and turns a long-mission ordinance bottleneck into additional control/damage. It can also hand a grenade to a friendly VIP in niche situations.
  • Savior vs Chaining Jolt: Casey keeps at least one high-end healer; Andy now chooses Chaining Jolt because multi-target guaranteed damage gives the Specialist productive combat turns. Disassembly in the XCOM row pushes even harder toward Chaining Jolt.
  • Full Override is mandatory-tier in both philosophies. Permanent MEC control can decide Forge, Psi Gate and Avenger Defense; even simple repeated shutdowns make the Specialist more active over a multi-turn fight.
  • Restoration vs Capacitor Discharge: Casey values Restoration as disaster insurance; Andy prefers Capacitor Discharge for guaranteed AoE damage. If the barracks repeatedly needs Restoration, also examine the tactical cause of the wounds.
  • Hacking requires squad support. Stack Skulljack/Skullmining Hack bonuses with Redscreen/Bluescreen effects; a higher-tier MEC takeover can swing an entire mission. Skirmisher Manual Override is exceptionally strong with Haywire/Combat Protocol cooldowns.
  • Small-squad warning: a pure support Specialist can be dead weight in a 4-person combat team. If the objective does not need remote hacking and the team cannot afford low direct damage, take a more offensive class.
  • Loadout: upgraded Gremlin is crucial; Skulljack + Medikit + plating is the classic support kit. SMG is the mobility default, rifle only for good-Aim/mobile or deliberate Overwatch builds. Protect the Specialist because they are often the only soldier who can restore another disabled/wounded unit.
Casey 1.2 Specialist edge cases not to miss
  • Scanning Protocol charge discipline: early Gremlins give too few charges to scan casually. Casey normally saves the first scan until after the ambush, takes a full blue move to maximize the scan footprint, and values the third charge from the next Gremlin tier because it makes Specialist-primary scouting practical. Later tiers can add still more charges.
  • Revival alternative: every soldier has the emergency Get Up interaction for an unconscious ally, but it consumes both actions. That makes Revival Protocol less mandatory while still leaving it valuable during early Chosen/stun-heavy missions.
  • Blinding Protocol is stronger than a normal Flashbang in one niche: it ignores resistance and can be used on an inactive pod without activating it. It is still competing with the information value of Scanning Protocol.
  • Trojan control timing: the improved shutdown duration lets a controlled robot contribute for another turn before you deliberately dispose of it, rather than ending control with a live enemy ready to act.
  • Failsafe nuance: it can justify risky tower hacks with ugly penalties, but on full-salvage/Troop Ambush maps an intentionally failed security hack can sometimes summon additional corpse-bearing reinforcements—Failsafe removes that upside along with the risk.
  • Overwatch Specialist commitment: one shooter perk is not enough. The archetype wants Sentinel + Ever Vigilant + Cool Under Pressure + later Kill Zone support, Rifle, strong magazine and Aim. Casey’s point is that the build works only when followed through.
  • Kill Zone equipment consequence: once the Overwatch route reaches Kill Zone, Expanded Magazine becomes more important than the otherwise-serviceable Autoloader because ammunition itself becomes the limiting resource.
  • Capacitor Discharge is attractive with Disassembly because it gives a low-damage class another guaranteed-AoE action, but it is limited-use; Casey could not verify the exact stun probability and does not treat the stun as guaranteed.

SMG + plating + grenade-heavy utility. Prioritize Incendiary/Sting payloads and launcher upgrades; EXO can add long-mission endurance. Shooting mods are low priority; Mobility/defensive PCS usually matter more.

Complete source notes — Grenadier
  • Role progression: early guaranteed damage → mid/late control. Incendiary, Sting and Bluescreen effects are the core reasons the class remains valuable after raw grenade damage stops scaling.
  • 1.2 moved extra grenade capacity from perks into launcher tiers. Launcher upgrades are therefore class power upgrades, not optional weapon housekeeping.
  • Needle vs Sapper: Casey changed from Sapper to Needle because +damage and corpse preservation matter early; Sapper remains reliable cover destruction and Andy likes it conceptually but finds it less consistent than expected. The decision is damage/corpse preservation versus guaranteed terrain destruction.
  • Rapid Deployment/Salvo warning: action economy is seductive but can make the Grenadier spend two of only 3–4 meaningful consumables in one encounter. Casey’s practical model is roughly one major grenade per encounter during the months where a Grenadier has only 3–4 charges and a normal mission contains several pods/RNF events. Both authors increasingly prefer making each grenade stronger rather than throwing them faster.
  • Early on, Grenadiers should usually carry damaging grenades; other classes can throw Flashbangs far enough. Later, the Grenadier becomes the natural Flashbang carrier because Sting/Bluescreen/launcher upgrades make those effects uniquely powerful.
  • Bombardier solves a practical range problem. Two tiles often decides whether an Incendiary/Sting can reach the Muton Elite/MEC that actually matters. Sapper becomes less valuable once control, rather than cover removal, dominates the job.
  • Bluescreen Bombs are not mainly a damage perk. They can move Specialist hack odds up roughly one mechanical tier without reducing the future puppet’s HP; remember that hacking a disoriented robot removes disorientation.
  • Sting Grenades are top-tier control, especially into multi-robot pods. The sequence “Sting/Bluescreen → Hack best MEC → kill easy targets” can neutralize otherwise overwhelming late pods.
  • Volatile Mix materially increases the number of enemies caught by Incendiary/Sting effects; Casey’s examples are a basic Incendiary expanding from a plus-shaped footprint to roughly a 3×3 area, with bomb-tier effects becoming much larger. The class transitions from “grenade damage” to “large-area status application.”
  • Shockwave is support on top of offense, reducing defense/dodge for follow-up shooters. Andy is less enthusiastic because the class is often throwing Flashbang/Incendiary effects when shooters may focus on other targets.
  • SMG and Mobility: Grenadiers almost never need premium primary-weapon damage. High HP is valuable because utility slots compete with plating. On large missions Andy sometimes drops plating for one more grenade, but no longer does this automatically.
  • Advanced launcher slot: put a high-value limited grenade such as Incendiary/Frost into the duplicated slot to stretch the rare item, and use regular slots for Flashbangs/mission payloads.
  • Heavy-weapon armor can give one extra high-impact action after grenades run low. Specialist Airdrop is an especially strong partner for medium/long missions.
Casey 1.2 Grenadier edge cases not to miss
  • Ordnance math explains the anti-Salvo stance: for much of the campaign a plated Grenadier carries only about 3–4 meaningful grenades while a normal mission often has roughly four combat encounters. Spending two consumables in one turn can leave the class with no premium action for the next pod.
  • Shooter Grenadier is possible, not preferred. A randomly very high-Aim Grenadier with multiple strong XCOM-row shooter perks can make Holo/CUP/Sentinel-style choices coherent, but the opportunity cost is weaker grenade/control output.
  • Sapper detail: its extra environmental damage makes many common 5–10-HP cover objects reliably destructible when the grenade is centered correctly. Its value falls later as Incendiary/Sting control replaces cover destruction as the class’s main job.
  • Bluescreen Bombs commonly move Hack odds by roughly one mechanical tier; Casey’s practical examples often show around a ~30-point improvement, but the exact increase depends on target defenses.
  • Volatile Mix geometry matters: it can turn the small Incendiary Grenade footprint into a full 3×3-style area and makes Incendiary Bomb coverage dramatically larger, often catching extra enemies rather than merely increasing damage.
  • Aggression does not improve grenade crits even if Biggest Booms is selected. Do not buy it expecting an explosives interaction.
  • Casey’s preferred XCOM-row rolls are Flashbanger and Demolition; Shellshock is useful, while defensive rolls are acceptable fallback AP sinks when the lottery does not offer grenade synergies.

Complete source notes — Gunner
  • Primary identity: boss/mechanical killer. Shred first, then multi-shot with Bluescreen so the rest of the squad attacks a lower-armor target.
  • Andy has progressively moved away from suppression-centric builds. Suppression remains useful, especially versus Chosen, but hard control or outright kills are preferred when available.
  • Center Mass vs Grazing Fire: grazes can preserve Chain Shot’s second shot and still apply shred/ammo effects; Center Mass produces more consistent kill thresholds. Andy has moved back toward Center Mass because one-HP survivors occur more often than graze saves a turn.
  • Formidable is boring but valuable because early wound recovery and survivability matter. Flush is substantially better than in older versions and can function like a high-Aim finisher/Chosen reposition tool.
  • Shredder is central because the Gunner is already reserving the ammo slot for Bluescreen; substituting Shredder Rounds would create an equipment conflict.
  • Chain Shot is the first easy multi-shot tool and remains relevant all campaign. Demolition belongs on someone else if the Gunner is your best direct shooter.
  • Cyclic Fire is the signature single-target boss attack; three Bluescreen shots can delete or heavily cripple late mechanicals.
  • Kill Zone gives the class a second identity: devastating first-contact/ambush fire. Rapid Fire is usable but overlaps with Chain Shot and has a meaningful aim penalty on a class whose Aim is lower than Ranger.
  • MSGT choice: Saturation Fire is situational but explosive into rainbow pods; Traverse Fire provides reliable action economy; Combat Fitness repairs bad Mobility/Aim. Andy prefers Saturation if Kill Zone was skipped, otherwise all three are legitimate.
  • XCOM-row reaction perks such as Ready For Anything / Ever Vigilant / Rapid Reaction can justify an alternate reaction-fire package. This is the main reason to depart from the boss-killer baseline.
  • Loadout: Scope is premium because multi-shot abilities carry aim penalties. Bluescreen becomes the default ammo. Remaining slots are plating/vest according to survivability.
Casey 1.2 Gunner edge cases not to miss
  • Full suppression alternative: Lockdown + Mayhem + Cool Under Pressure can turn suppression into an offensive reaction-fire engine. It is strongest when XCOM can force suppressed enemies to move by removing/flanking cover; Casey still prefers directed fire because enemies often simply stay put.
  • Flush against reaction enemies: using Flush on Chosen/Rulers can force their reaction to be movement rather than a damaging action; against the Assassin it can pull her away from the next victim. This is a control use, not primarily a finisher.
  • Hail of Bullets remains a powerful guaranteed-hit tool, but arrives after the campaign already has more ways to solve cover and it competes with Shredder, which every late squad needs from somewhere.
  • Demolition reliably deletes even sturdy destructible cover, but can choose the wrong cover element when the target touches two objects. More importantly, using your best shooter to expose a target is only good if another shooter is available to exploit it.
  • Danger Zone adds only a small radius increase, but that matters because enemy spacing is often just wide enough to defeat standard Area Suppression.
  • Kill Zone + incendiary opener is a double-ambush package: use reaction fire while a Technical/Grenadier controls the same large pod. Casey also particularly values Kill Zone on Alien Ruler activation.
  • Traverse Fire + Rapid Fire can create Ranger-like repeated directed fire every turn, but those perks arrive late and overlap with Chain Shot/Cyclic Fire that the boss-killer already owns.
Technical — Rocketeer 1.2 revised
ANDY 1.2.2Napalm X → Roust → Burnout → Phosphorus → Incinerator → Quickburn → Bunker Buster / Firestorm.

This is the largest Casey/Andy split: Casey’s current 1.2 route is Rocketeer; Andy prefers pure flame/control after repeated Legend campaigns.

SMG + Gauntlet + plating. Rocketeer: preserve rocket capacity and consider heavy-weapon armor later. Burner: mobility and durable medium/heavy armor matter more. Primary-gun upgrades are secondary.

  • Salvo: can support two-rocket turns; Quickburn remains a legitimate alternative if you value move → burn → rocket action economy.
  • Bunker Buster: huge area, mass cover destruction, strong end-tree payoff.
  • Burner alternatives remain viable: Napalm-X greatly improves flamethrower control, Incinerator expands arc size/range, High Pressure Tank/Roust add charges.

  • Squad construction is about roles, not class quotas. High-priority roles are reliable killer, control, officer, scouting/information, repeatable shooting, hack/objective access, boss damage/shred and cover destruction. Healer/tank/Overwatch become more mission-specific.

  • Common givers: concealed Shinobi, Specialist, Grenadier and some Gunners/Holobots. Common takers: Ranger, Assault, Gunner, Reaper, Templar and other high-value attackers. Psi/Sharpshooter are often more neutral because their turn is already structured around two useful actions.

Who should become the officer?

ANDY

Shinobi officer

Andy trains essentially every Shinobi as an officer. A concealed scout often has spare tactical actions, so spending those actions on Command / Focus Fire / Get Some does not sacrifice your primary damage dealer's turn. His normal path is Focus Fire → Get Some → Trial By Fire → Collector → Defilade → Infiltrator → Scavenger/Combined Arms.

CASEY

Specialist rose in value in 1.2

Older Casey advice favored Shinobi officers. His 1.2 Specialist update makes Specialist a much stronger officer candidate because Scanning Protocol can provide information while the Shinobi is freed to fight. The exact candidate therefore depends on whether your officer should primarily scout or support.

SYNTHESIS For a first campaign, Shinobi officer is the simplest default because the action economy is intuitive and matches Andy's tested routine. A high-stat Specialist or Holo Sharpshooter can justify a different tree, especially if you want Lead By Example. Do not force the same officer class into every squad if it creates a role gap.

Officer operational rules

  • Bring an officer on almost every normal mission if roster depth permits; the action economy from Command is valuable immediately.
  • Do not stack officers expecting multiple officer kits: only the mission leader's officer abilities function.
  • Command cannot target SPARKs, so a SPARK-heavy squad reduces one of the officer's strongest tactical tools.
  • Keep the officer close enough for Command Range perks when you need them; many aura perks do not buff the officer themself.
  • Officer rank also helps a soldier Haven Adviser expose Faceless faster.
  • Leadership familiarity bonuses are real but slow. Build squads around mission requirements first; treat repeat-officer bonuses as a bonus, not a constraint.
